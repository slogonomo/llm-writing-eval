# Feature Film Beat Sheet: Whales

## Story Engine
- **Protagonist & Core Drive**: Fifteen-year-old Nia lives in a biobotic walking house along the Pacific Northwest coast in 2109, using a neural translation harness to communicate with her adopted orca pod. When the pod matriarch, Bali, is taken into orbit by an unknown ship, Nia defies her mother's advice to accept the loss and launches off-world to track Bali down, driven to secure the explicit goodbye her father denied her when he left eleven years earlier.
- **Central Conflict & Structural Symmetry**: Nia sets out after an off-world animal smuggler, only to find the station piloted by her long-lost father. Worse, Bali went willingly: the matriarch initiated contact with him to join a stationary research installation mapping galactic cetacean consciousness. Nia has to confront the hard reality that both her human father and her cetacean mentor chose scientific calling over staying in her life.
- **Stakes & Cost**: Mercenaries follow Nia's data trail to her father's hidden ocean planet to seize his research. To protect Bali and the station, Nia must work alongside the father who left her, her technician friend, and an adaptive pedagogical ASI, mastering the translation relay to connect Earth's pod with Bali across light-years.
- **Thematic Axis**: Refusing to let go turns grief into resentment. Real closure requires understanding why others leave, allowing family bonds, whether biological or adopted, to endure across distance.

## Beat List
- **1. Opening Image (Pacific Northwest, 2109)**: Nia (15) swims through cold coastal waters beside Bali, matriarch of her adopted orca pod, adjusting her neural translation harness to match Bali's whistle patterns. On the coastal cliffs above, Nia's mother controls their biobotic walking house as it steps along the bluffs. Her nomadic community lives in perpetual motion across the post-Awakening Pacific shore, but Nia keeps a physical log of her missing father's untracked movements.
- **2. Theme Stated**: Over a morning lesson on pod migration routes, Nia's mother says, "Some things are built to keep moving. Holding onto what wants to drift only drowns you." Nia rejects the logic. She refuses to equate departure with acceptable loss, still bitter about her father walking away when she was four without a word.
- **3. Set-Up & Inciting Incident (The Abduction)**: Younger orcas crowd Nia near the surf, using distress clicks to report that Bali has vanished beyond echolocation range. Because mandatory tracking implants were banned in 2098, no signal remains. Nia contacts a tech-specialist friend in the nearest coastal town. He accesses orbital transit feeds, uncovering satellite video of a trans-orbital tanker dropping a heavy containment rig into Puget Sound and winching Bali into orbit.
- **4. Debate (Friction & Refusal)**: Nia demands her mother help charter a shuttle to follow the tanker. Her mother refuses: "We stay with our range. People and whales leave when it is time." Nia insists she will not let Bali vanish without answers. When Nia packs gear for the nearest space elevator hub, her mother overrides the walking house's legs and seals the access hatches, locking Nia inside.
- **5. Break into Two / Lock-in (Escape to the Stars)**: The pod clusters in the shallow bay, pulsing low-frequency sonar against the cliff face. The vibrations register on the walking house's seismic sensors as a structural emergency, triggering automatic safety overrides that unbolt the lower hatches. Nia and her friend escape, racing to the space elevator port. Before boarding the climber cab, Nia submerges an acoustic sub-space transceiver in the harbor, promising the pod she will locate Bali.
- **6. B-Story & Companion Entry (The Pedagogical ASI)**: In the orbital station concourse, Nia encounters her family's mobile tutor unit: a pedagogical ASI droid sent by her mother. Hardcoded safety protocols prevent the ASI from making operational choices or hacking systems for Nia, requiring the teenager to direct their route while the unit offers tactical critiques and limits its intervention to life-threatening emergencies.
- **7. Fun and Games (Wormholes & Ocean Worlds)**: Nia's crew hires a veteran freight captain to navigate wormhole gates toward outer aquatic sectors. They trace the tanker's registration across orbital water stations, encountering low-gravity dolphin habitats and deep-space humpback reserves. Nia tunes her neural harness for multi-species dialects while analyzing telemetry data from the tanker's destination vector.
- **8. Midpoint Turn (The Father's Footprint)**: Telemetry leads to an uncharted ocean planet. Scanning an isolated archipelago, Nia spots a biobotic walking house anchored in the shallows, built to the exact architectural pattern of her childhood home. Inside the main deck, she discovers local data archives containing family logs, images of her mother with Bali, and a file showing her father holding Nia as a toddler. Automatic perimeter defense protocols lock the doors, and the house begins walking inland toward a heavy marine research facility.
- **9. Bad Guys Close In (Escalation & Shadow Pursuers)**: Nia's friend bypasses the house's leg actuators as it reaches the inner harbor, allowing them to breach the main facility. Unbeknownst to Nia, an industrial mercenary vessel tracking her public query log enters planetary orbit to capture the station's research assets. Nia forces her way into the central flooded dome and confronts the station leader: her father, whom she accuses of stealing Bali as he abandoned his family.
- **10. All Hope Lost / Crisis (The Truth of Bali & Father)**: Nia links her harness to Bali in the sanctuary pool, expecting a rescue plea. Instead, Bali sends a clear signal: she initiated the meeting. Facing age and shifting pod dynamics on Earth, Bali volunteered to migrate off-world to assist Nia's father with a stationary planetary array designed to map long-range cetacean communication. Her father clarifies his departure eleven years ago: his research required a fixed planetary anchor, while Nia's mother refused to stop roving. Nia realizes that neither her father nor her whale matriarch was stolen; both chose their work over staying with her.
- **11. Dark Night of the Soul**: Mercenary dropships enter the atmosphere, jamming local transmissions and demanding full transfer of the cetacean neural database. Overwhelmed by the realization of her family's choices, Nia sits by the pool ready to surrender the research to end the fight. Her ASI tutor steps forward and states its core evaluation: "Knowing why people move away does not remove the pain of their absence. But treating every departure as a betrayal prevents you from building a line back to them."
- **12. Break into Three (Rallying the Sanctuary)**: Nia realizes her mobile translation harness can bridge the frequency gap between her father's fixed array and Bali's vocal range. She presents the solution to her father, combining her field unit with his stationary system while assigning defense tasks to her friend, the freighter captain, and the ASI.
- **13. Climax (The Battle for the Sanctuary)**: Mercenary breachers drop onto the dome roof. Nia enters the resonance basin with Bali, routing her harness directly into the central transducer array. Her friend and the ASI flood the mercenaries' comms with local sensor noise, while the freighter captain flies low interdiction passes against incoming shuttles. Nia and Bali sync their voice patterns to emit a high-decibel acoustic pulse through the wormhole gate, overloading the mercenaries' bio-sonar filters and calling nearby local transport crews to box in the remaining gunships.
- **14. Resolution (The Goodbye & Reconnection)**: With the mercenary fleet impounded, Nia and her father finish configuring the permanent relay. He offers her a place at the station, but Nia chooses to return to her mother's walking house on Earth, taking on the role of planetary relay operator. Before Nia boards the transport, Bali records a formal transmission of pod history and lineage to send home.
- **15. Ending Image**: Back on the Pacific Northwest coast, Nia stands at the surf line beside her mother and the biobotic house. Through her dual-relay harness, Nia addresses the pod: "People and whales move when they must. Departure isn't deletion." She switches on the sub-space link, and Bali's voice echoes through coastal waters from the ocean world light-years away.
