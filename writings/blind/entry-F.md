# Whales: Feature Film Beat Sheet

> From John's story notes (2026-08-08). Working logline: *After her pod's matriarch is captured, a nomadic 15-year-old whale-talker defies her mother and journeys off-world in pursuit, only to discover the captor is the father who abandoned her to put down roots.*

## Story Engine

- **Protagonist**: Nia, 15, whale-talker who has been improving the human–orca translation system since she was 8; belongs to a nomadic community that lives in walking biobotic houses led by her mother, the houses' original designer.
- **External goal**: Find and bring home Bali, the pod matriarch who was scooped from the Pacific by a tanker — before the trail goes cold. If she fails, her chosen family loses its matriarch and she loses the only place she has ever belonged.
- **Internal cost**: Defying her mother ("the person who means the most, holding us back like a bad tide"), leaving her community, and eventually facing the father who abandoned her without goodbye — the person who knows exactly how to wound her.
- **Engine**: Quest pursuit (chase across worlds, collecting lore about a mysterious ship and its pilot) with a **lie-to-truth** secondary that takes over in Act 3: Nia believes the captor is a monster, and must watch that belief disintegrate: the matriarch *chose* to go, and the captor is family.
- **Stakes / theme**: True hurt only comes from someone we love; leaving is not the same as gone; family is both born and adopted. The matriarch's abandonment mirrors the father's, and Nia must decide whether absence can still be a kind of connection.

## Beat List

### Act One: The Missing Matriarch (establish the two families; lock the goal)

- **B1. Two families, one girl (opening image).** Nia at home in the water with the orca pod: translation flowing, learning and work integrated, a window turning into a display of orcas at play. *Track: internal* — establish the twin loyalties (mother's walking-house community above; pod below). *Changes: relationship, knowledge (world rules)*.
- **B2. The pod comes to her (inciting incident).** Disturbed pod members gather around Nia: Bali is missing, days now, invisible to even long-range echolocation. Because Nia is the one who talks to them, she is the only human who can hear the emergency. *Changes: pressure, knowledge*. *Therefore* →
- **B3. The tanker (investigation).** Nia's own scanners find nothing (forced tracking chips were outlawed when orca sentience was recognized in 2098). Her enhanced-techie friend, from outside the community, pulls satellite footage: a ship descending, scooping Bali in, separating her from the pod. Deliberate abduction, not accident. *Changes: knowledge* — the goal becomes chaseable. *Therefore* →
- **B4. "Sometimes people just have to go" (confrontation).** Nia tells her mother; her mother refuses: "We'll be moving on soon, and so will they." Nia: "Not without saying goodbye." First crack in the mother's authority; Nia sees the bad tide for what it is. *Track: internal*. *Changes: relationship, choice availability*.
- **B5. The escape (end of Act One / point of no return).** Mother confines Nia; the whales help her slip away; Nia and the techie friend take the space elevator to the orbital colony. She gives the pod a communicator — "if they want it," they can turn it on. The goodbye she was denied, she performs anyway. *Changes: pressure, choice* — external goal locked, home forfeited.

### Act Two: The Trail of a Ghost (escalate the chase; begin dismantling the lie)

- **B6. The embodied ASI (comic relief, rules of the game).** Her mother's resident ASI catches up: superintelligent, never allowed to give the answers, tags along for everything. It adds friction, humor, and a running constraint: the kids must figure it out themselves. *Changes: relationship (new crew member), knowledge (world rule)*.
- **B7. The spacer (the guide).** A smarmy old spacer, who knows which worlds can support whales, agrees to track the tanker. Establishes the space-whale ecosystem: most don't travel (humpbacks stay), dolphins get everywhere. *Changes: knowledge* — the chase now has range.
- **B8. Worlds of misadventure (escalating fragments).** Multiple worlds, local whales, collected lore about the mysterious ship and the man who flies it. Each world adds a piece — and a hint that doesn't fit "monster": the pilot is described with care, precision, protection. *Track: external forward, internal tilting*. *Changes: knowledge, pressure*.
- **B9. Midpoint turn — the walking house.** The trail ends on an unnamed world, and Nia sees a walking house like her own. She's never seen one elsewhere. Someone from her community is here. The enemy is one of her own people. *Changes: knowledge, pressure* — the chase and the family wound collide.
- **B10. The pictures (the reveal).** Empty house, then photographs: her mother, the matriarch, baby pictures — and one of the three of them: Nia at four, her mother young, and *him*. The man who flies the ship is the father who didn't say goodbye. *Track: internal*. *Changes: knowledge* — the lie is fully armed, ready to die.
- **B11. "He arrives" (emotional confrontation).** Confusion, resentment; Nia storms out. The reunion is a wound, not a homecoming. *Changes: relationship, pressure*.
- **B12. Pursuers (escalation — her own trail bites back).** The industrialists who have been hunting the father's work followed *Nia's* trail across worlds. His project is now threatened — not by her, but because of her. Her quest has become the danger. *Changes: pressure, choice* — she must now protect what she came to take.
- **B13. The big confrontation (the lie dies).** Nia has secured her prize. She is about to take the whale. "You don't understand," he says. "She wanted to come here. She wants to be part of this." The matriarch contacted *him*. The symmetry lands: the matriarch abandoned her family exactly as Nia's father abandoned her. *Track: internal climax of Act Two*. *Changes: knowledge, relationship* — everything re-frames.
- **B14. The "this" (why he left).** His work: a fixed deep-space bridge project that must sit still, while her mother's work always required moving. He left not to escape Nia, but because the two callings were incompatible, and the work was here. It was never about her. *Changes: knowledge, internal* — the abandonment wound is re-diagnosed.

### Act Three: The Symmetry Resolved (external climax; internal closure)

- **B15. The triumphant confrontation (climax).** The industrialists close in; Nia and her friends help her father and the matriarch defeat them and carry the project to its next stage. She defends the family she came to dismantle. *Track: external*. *Changes: pressure peaks, then releases; choice made*.
- **B16. The speech (the echo).** Nia returns home and gives the whale-children a jumbled, touching speech, also to herself: "Sometimes people just have to go… but that doesn't mean they're gone." She gives the matriarch a communicator: the Act One gift, now a permanent bridge between worlds. *Track: internal*. *Changes: relationship, knowledge* — the goal question is answered: she couldn't bring Bali home, but she can make absence livable.
- **B17. The matriline continues (final image).** The granddaughter's end-of-movie pregnancy: the matriarch's line carries on; the pod is not ending, it's branching. Nia in the water with the pod, the communicator alive, her mother watching from the house. Leaving isn't gone; the families hold. *Changes: relationship, closure*. Bookends B1.

## Directional Picks (from John's open questions — flagged, not locked)

- **Way to space**: space elevator + orbital colony — chosen because it gives Nia a discoverable trail and a hub where a spacer could catch wind of the tanker.
- **FTL**: wormholes, per John's lean; the colony doubles as a wormhole terminal.
- **The father's work ("this")**: a long-range communication/bridge project with non-terrestrial intelligence (the space-whales' kin) that must be anchored at one fixed point, which is why the father had to sit still. Industrialists want the tech. *Needs John's pick from his list; this is the directional fill.*
- **Why no contact**: the work's hidden nature — the industrialists were hunting it, so contact would have exposed the location and endangered the matriarch and the project. Supported by the notes' own reasoning.
- **The father-reveal variant**: primary path is "he arrives" (B11). The alternate (the house trapping them and walking inland toward a fortress) is a viable Act Two midpoint replacement (B9/B10) if the beat sheet needs more physical threat before the emotional confrontation; it would delay the reveal and add a set-piece escape. Not locked.
- **Orca matriline**: folded in as B17 (granddaughter pregnancy) to land the "matriline splitting" theme with joy.

## Self-Check (vs rb.outline)

- **Causality**: passed. Every beat is linked by "therefore/but": B2's discovery forces B3's investigation, B4's refusal forces B5's escape, B10's reveal forces B11's confrontation, B12 is caused *by* B14's hidden-work premise (her trail leads the hunters), B16 pays off B5's communicator.
- **Escalation**: passed. Pressure climbs through the investigation → escape → multi-world chase → midpoint home-house reveal → confrontation → pursuers → climax; internal escalation runs parallel (authority crack → abandonment wound → lie dies → re-diagnosis).
- **Closure**: passed. The external goal (bring Bali home) is answered: Bali chooses to stay, and Nia converts the goal into connection; the theme (leaving ≠ gone) lands in B16–B17 and bookends B1.
- **Fit**: passed. Quest structure family with lie-to-truth secondary is the project's actual engine (chase + belief collapse), and the beat sheet exposes both tracks rather than forcing a generic 15-beat template.
- **Borderline**: B8 (worlds of misadventure) risks becoming filler if each world doesn't add a distinct lore fragment *and* a hint that re-shapes the pilot's identity — it's the segment that will sag first and is the prime candidate for sequence-tightening in outline.
- **Likely next step**: the beat sheet holds; the next pass is an outline that sequences B8's misadventures and decides the B9/B10 variant (house trap vs. he arrives).
