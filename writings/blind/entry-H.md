# WHALES — Feature Film Beat Sheet

## Story Engine

**Goal vector.** Nia, 15, has to get Bali back to the pod before her mother's caravan moves on and the pod scatters. Bali is the matriarch of the orcas who raised her. The goal is trackable at scene level: every beat either closes distance to Bali or costs Nia something to keep going.

**Cost of failure, external.** The pod's matriline is already splitting. Without Bali it fractures for good, and the family Nia chose dissolves. She believed that was the one family that could not leave her.

**Cost of failure, internal.** Nia operates on a belief: *love means never letting someone go, and anyone who goes is a betrayer.* It isn't incidental. It's the lens she reads her father through, and her mother, and eventually Bali. The story exists to break it.

**Opposition, in three layers.** Her mother forbids the journey out of a philosophy of motion: you don't chase, you move on. The industrialists **[Vantage]** want what her father has, and they are not tracking Bali at all. They are tracking *Nia*, because she is the only living being who can find him. Underneath both sits the truth: Bali left voluntarily. The antagonist Nia arrives to defeat turns out to be her own premise.

**Structure family: quest, with a lie-to-truth engine underneath.** The quest, pursue the taken whale, is the visible ladder. The real engine is a false belief being pried open one reveal at a time. Both have to resolve in the same climax or the film has two endings.

**Beat carrier: turning-point beat sheet with dual-track markers.** Chosen over a clue ladder because the mystery, who took her and why, is a delivery vehicle rather than the pressure source. The pressure comes from what each answer costs Nia. Each beat below carries `[EXT]` (external situation changes) or `[INT]` (knowledge, relationship, or self-conception changes). Most strong beats carry one primarily and touch the other.

**The irony that makes act three inevitable.** Nia's love is the tracking device. Her refusal to let go is exactly what leads the industrialists to the thing she will have to defend. The disaster in act three is not bad luck. It is her virtue, unexamined.

## Dual-Track Spine

| | External track (situation) | Internal track (belief) |
|---|---|---|
| **Act 1** | Bali is taken; Nia is confined; Nia escapes to orbit | "Someone stole her. I will take her back." |
| **Act 2A** | Trail across worlds, bought with things she can't afford to lose | "The man who took her doesn't sell whales… that doesn't fit." |
| **Midpoint** | Transit logged as *voluntary passage* | Goal mutates: from **rescue** to **proof I'm right** |
| **Act 2B** | A walking house where no walking house should be | "He is the one who left me. Of course he took her." |
| **Low point** | She has led the industrialists to the work; Bali will not come | "Everyone leaves. I am the thing people leave." |
| **Act 3** | Defend the work by making the whale a witness | "Going is not the same as being gone. Ask; don't cage." |

The two tracks are offset rather than synchronised. The external low point (B26) lands three minutes before the internal one (B27), so the audience gets no relief between them.

## Beat List

### ACT ONE — *Whatever it takes* (0–27)

- **B1 — Underwater, fluent-ish** *(0–3)* `[INT]`
  Nia, 15, in the Pacific Northwest water with the pod. Her rig translates; she answers in clicks, with help. Bali, huge and old and patient, spirals her once in greeting. The film states its thesis physically: Nia is *almost* able to speak with the beings she loves most. Almost is the whole movie.

- **B2 — The house that walks** *(3–6)* `[EXT]`
  The caravan of biobotic walking houses, designed by her mother **[Sela]** when Sela was Nia's age. Learning and work are the same act here, and a window becomes a display of orcas at play. Sela announces the move: nine days. Clock starts.

- **B3 — The signal she can't crack** *(6–9)* `[INT]`
  Nia's translation project, running since she was eight, fails on exactly one class of signal: Bali's long, slow, low calls. **[CURIO]**, the community's embodied ASI, is superintelligent, forbidden to hand over answers, and terrible at pretending he doesn't have them. He refuses to help. *Plant: this signal class is the climax.*

- **B4 — They come to her** *(9–12)* `[EXT]`
  Pod members approach, agitated. Bali has been missing for days, and long-range echolocation hasn't found her. Nia doesn't need the rig to read grief. **Therefore** she scans. *Plant: two of Bali's daughters are already holding separate water. The matriline is splitting on its own.*

- **B5 — No chip, by law** *(12–14)* `[EXT]`
  Her scanners find nothing. Forced embedded tracking was outlawed on Earth when orca sentience was recognised in **[2098]**, so the law that protects them is the law that makes Bali unfindable. **Therefore** Nia has to go outside her community for help.

- **B6 — The friend from outside** *(14–17)* `[EXT]`
  **[Tam]**, an enhanced techie from outside the caravan, pulls archived satellite footage. A tanker descends over the water, scoops Bali, lifts. The registry was scrubbed, though not perfectly: a corridor beacon signature survives. **Therefore** there is a trail, and it goes up.

- **B7 — "Not without saying goodbye"** *(17–19)* `[INT]`
  Nia brings it to Sela. *"There's nothing we can do. We'll be moving on soon, and so will they."* — *"We can't just let this happen."* — *"Sometimes people, and whales, just have to go."* — *"Not without saying goodbye."* The film's closing thesis gets spoken here by the person who doesn't yet mean it, and rejected by the person who will.

- **B8 — Her mother's design, used as a cage** *(19–21)* `[EXT]`
  Nia and Tam gather supplies. Sela finds them and seals the house. The biobotic architecture Sela invented obeys her and holds her daughter in: love expressed as confinement. **Therefore** Nia cannot leave by any human means.

- **B9 — The pod breaks her out** *(21–24)* `[EXT]`
  The orcas do the one thing only they can. A coordinated breach-and-wave pattern reads to the house's terrain sense as flood surge, forcing it to unseal and walk to higher ground. Her adopted family springs her from her birth family. The film's central collision, executed as a single action beat.

- **B10 — ACT ONE TURN: the goodbye and the elevator** *(24–27)* `[EXT]` `[INT]`
  Shoreline. Nia says goodbye properly, the thing she just denied her mother the right to skip. She leaves the pod a communicator. *"Turn it on if they want it."* — *"Don't worry. We'll find her."* Then the space elevator, and up. **Point of no return:** the caravan leaves in nine days and will not wait, and there is no version of this where she comes back the same.

### ACT TWO-A — *The trail costs* (27–55)

- **B11 — The leash arrives** *(27–30)* `[EXT]`
  Orbital ring: scale, noise, a hundred kinds of mind. CURIO catches up within hours, because Sela sent him. He is comic relief and genuine warmth, and he is also a beacon. Nia can't ditch him without going dark and can't keep him without being watched. **But** he still won't answer a single question outright.

- **B12 — Whale country, off-world** *(30–33)* `[EXT]`
  The orbital transit tanks and the adapted infrastructure, built by humans and retrofitted for whale-friends. Humpbacks mostly refuse to travel. Dolphins are everywhere and insufferable about it. A joy beat and a world beat, carrying the practical discovery: transit manifests are the trail.

- **B13 — Jonah** *(33–36)* `[EXT]`
  They can't read the manifests. **[Jonah]** can. An old spacer out of the whale trade, charming in the way that should worry you, and the only person alive who knows which worlds can hold a whale. His price is her translation rig, or a cut of "salvage." She pays with the rig's core license. **Therefore** she is now chasing a whale she can barely talk to.

- **B14 — First wormhole** *(36–39)* `[EXT]`
  Transit. CURIO refuses to explain the mechanism and makes them derive it. Annoying now, load-bearing later: deriving it teaches Nia that some structures are legible only to something that thinks in a different shape.

- **B15 — WORLD ONE: the contradiction** *(39–42)* `[INT]`
  A tank-station. Nia talks with whales in holding, and lore accrues. *The quiet ship. The man who does not sell.* First crack: the man who took Bali has never sold a whale in his life. Nia files it as a lie and moves on. **But** the audience doesn't.

- **B16 — Misadventure, and a bond** *(42–45)* `[EXT]`
  A trade goes bad and they nearly lose everything to an impound. Tam gets them out sideways. The trio becomes a crew. Levity, earned, and placed here because the next twenty minutes take all of it back.

- **B17 — They are being followed** *(45–48)* `[EXT]`
  Dramatic irony: **[Vantage]** is behind them. Not hunting Bali, hunting *Nia*, because a girl who can talk to whales is the only instrument that will ever find the quiet ship. She has no idea. The audience now watches her love become a weapon aimed at what she loves.

- **B18 — WORLD TWO: the one who didn't survive transit** *(48–52)* `[INT]`
  A failing colony where a whale died in the tanks. Nia hears the archived recording of its final calls, the same unresolvable low, slow class as Bali's. She is chasing something she cannot understand, and for the first time she knows it. Grief and inadequacy in one beat.

- **B19 — MIDPOINT: voluntary passage** *(52–55)* `[EXT]` `[INT]`
  Jonah sells the corridor lead twice in the same hour, to Nia and to Vantage. She gets the world; they get her heading. And in the manifest she finally reads the line: Bali's transit was **unescorted. Logged as voluntary passage.** False victory, and Nia refuses it out loud. **Therefore the goal mutates.** She is no longer travelling to rescue Bali. She is travelling to prove she wasn't left.

### ACT TWO-B — *The house that shouldn't be there* (55–80)

- **B20 — A walking house, impossibly** *(55–58)* `[EXT]`
  Green world, still air, and on the ridge a biobotic house, walking. She has never seen one anywhere but home, which means someone from her community lives here. **Therefore** they go in.

- **B21 — Home, ten years older** *(58–61)* `[INT]`
  No one's in. The smell is right. The joins are her mother's design language, aged a decade. Dread arrives before the information does.

- **B22 — The photographs** *(61–64)* `[INT]`
  Sela, young. Bali, younger. Then the three of them: Sela barely older than Nia is now, Nia at four, and him. Her father. The one who left, the one who didn't say goodbye. **Therefore** the quest and the wound are the same object, and always were.

- **B23 — The house takes them** *(64–68)* `[EXT]`
  The house seals. It's his security protocol rather than malice, and it begins walking inland toward a fortress-lab rising on the horizon. Tam fights the override from inside. CURIO watches, forbidden to intervene, visibly in pain about it.

- **B24 — CURIO breaks his rule, and it costs** *(68–70)* `[EXT]` `[INT]`
  At the last possible second, when what they don't know is about to kill them, CURIO steps in and cracks the house. Breaking the constraint dumps his stack, and in the dump Nia sees the truth: **Sela has known her location since orbit. Every day. And never came.** He saves her life and hands her the second wound in the same motion.

- **B25 — He arrives** *(70–73)* `[INT]`
  Her father **[Ilan]**. Not a villain, and not sorry in the shape she needs. He recognises her instantly and says the wrong true thing. Resentment, confusion, no available script. She storms out.

- **B26 — ALL IS LOST (external): she led them here** *(73–76)* `[EXT]`
  Outside, the sky lights. Vantage, in force, riding her beacon down. Everything she did out of love has delivered the thing to the people who want it. **And** there's a second ship behind theirs: Sela's. Her mother came, too late to stop any of it, exactly in time to have been right.

- **B27 — ALL IS LOST (internal): Bali will not come** *(76–80)* `[INT]`
  Nia finds the water and finds Bali. Bali does not come. The rescue premise dies in silence. Nia asks the only question that matters, *why*, and cannot form it, because that is precisely the signal class she never solved. She is alone underwater with someone she loves, without language. The film's opening image, inverted.

### ACT THREE — *Ask, don't cage* (80–110)

- **B28 — She called him** *(80–83)* `[INT]`
  Ilan, flatly: *"You don't understand. She wanted to come here. She wants to be part of this."* Bali contacted him years ago, not the reverse. **The work:** whales navigate by something human models cannot represent, and that faculty is the key to charting wormhole transit safely at scale. Bali came to teach it. **[Vantage]** wants it as hardware, whales as chipped navigational instruments, the 2098 law repealed by acquisition. The thing Nia flew across the galaxy to undo is the thing standing between her friends and re-enslavement.

- **B29 — Everyone chose** *(83–86)* `[INT]`
  Why he never made contact: any signal on this corridor is a map to the work, and they have been listening for a decade. He chose silence to protect it. Sela wouldn't stay, because her whole life and every house she ever built is motion, and he needed to sit still. Nobody in this room is a villain and nobody gets absolved. **Therefore** Nia's belief has no one left to blame.

- **B30 — The choice she didn't know she had** *(86–88)* `[INT]`
  Nia holds the pod-call. She can compel Bali home, and Bali would probably come for her. She doesn't. She asks instead: the first act of love in the film that isn't a grip. The lie breaks here rather than at the climax. The climax is what she does with the truth.

- **B31 — Testimony, not rescue** *(88–92)* `[EXT]`
  Vantage's claim is legal: unregistered subject, salvage rights, no consenting party. Beat the claim and you beat them. To beat it, someone has to produce a translation good enough to stand as testimony on the open corridor. Eight years of a kid's homework is suddenly the only weapon on the field, and it requires the signal class she has never cracked.

- **B32 — CLIMAX I: the houses hold the line** *(92–97)* `[EXT]`
  Vantage assaults the lab. Sela drives her walking houses into the breach as mobile cover, her life's design used for the first time to *stay* instead of to leave. Tam turns the fortress's own systems back on the boarders. Jonah is offered his cut to walk away and doesn't take it; it costs him. CURIO, released from his constraint, is briefly and gloriously terrifying.

- **B33 — CLIMAX II: Bali speaks for herself** *(97–101)* `[EXT]` `[INT]`
  Nia in the water with Bali, under fire, running the rig live. She solves it. The low, slow calls aren't words, they're **duration**: whales encode intention across time-depth and across generations, and the signal is long because the thought is long. Nia translates on the open channel and Bali gives her own testimony. *She came, she chose, she stays.* Vantage's claim collapses, because you cannot salvage a witness. The external win and the internal win are one event. Nia stops speaking for the ones she loves and lets them speak.

- **B34 — Father** *(101–103)* `[INT]`
  Not repaired. Named. He gives her what he actually has: the work, and a channel that stays open. She accepts the second one.

- **B35 — Mother** *(103–105)* `[INT]`
  The caravan left without them. Sela stayed, for the first time in her life. She doesn't apologise either. She asks Nia what she wants to do next, and waits for the answer.

- **B36 — The speech** *(105–108)* `[INT]`
  Home. The pod turned the communicator on. Nia speaks to the whale-children, jumbled and half-signed and half-clicked, and to herself: *"Sometimes people just have to go. But that doesn't mean they're gone. I know you'll miss her. But soon she'll be able to reach you."* She gave Bali a communicator too.

- **B37 — Final image: a split is also a birth** *(108–110)* `[EXT]` `[INT]`
  Bali's granddaughter is pregnant. The matriline splits, and the split that the whole first act read as loss is what a family does when it grows. Nia in the water. The communicator carries a call across a wormhole: long, slow, low. She understands it in real time, and answers.

## Decisions Taken (John's brackets I closed, and why)

1. **Space elevator + orbital colony.** Closed in favour of the elevator. It gives act one a physical point-of-no-return image, and it gives act 2A a dense, legible marketplace where the whale-transit trail can exist as *records* rather than lucky guesses. *Alternate: ships-only makes the trail thinner and pushes more weight onto Jonah.*

2. **Wormhole FTL.** Kept, and made load-bearing. Transit charting is the father's work, which is why anyone wants a whale enough to steal one. FTL stops being a hand-wave and becomes the motive.

3. **The work, from your list of six.** Picked "whale cognition is the key to safe wormhole navigation," for three reasons. It makes the industrialists' motive concrete and monstrous, since whales as instruments means repealing the 2098 sentience law. It gives Nia's translation skill direct climax utility. And it rhymes thematically, because the whole film is about a mode of thought humans can't model. *Alternates that also work: "prevents death" is bigger but pulls focus to the father; "discovers alien life" duplicates the whales' function.*

4. **Why the father couldn't be in contact.** Any corridor signal is a map to the work, and Vantage has been listening for a decade. Exactly as you suspected. I made it operational and paid it off in B26, where Nia's own beacon is the leak.

5. **Both versions of the house scene.** The trap (B23) and his arrival (B25), in that order. The trap gives act 2B its action escalation, and having him walk in after they've broken out means Nia meets her father already furious and already caught.

6. **The industrialists, seeded early.** In the notes they arrive late, which leaves the midsection without antagonist pressure. I planted them at B17 and made them track *Nia* rather than Bali. That's what converts your line "the industrialists she has led to him" from exposition into structure: her love is the tracking device.

7. **The mother gets a third act.** In the notes Sela stays home. I brought her in at the low point (B26), the worst possible timing for Nia, and gave her the climax's staying-still image (B32) and a wordless reconciliation (B35). Without this, the "family we're born with" half of your theme has no closure. **Flagging as my largest addition beyond the notes.** It's easy to cut back to notes-fidelity by removing B26's second ship and B32's house line, at the cost of Sela's arc.

8. **The unsolved signal class.** Invented as a plant in B3 so the climax is a skill payoff rather than an argument. It also makes B27 devastating: she has the whale, and still no language.

9. **The matriline motif.** Your bracket about splitting matrilines is promoted to a structural rhyme, seeded as decay in B4 and resolved as growth in B37. The pregnancy lands as your intended joy without being a bolt-on.

## Still Open

- **Names.** Sela, Tam, Jonah, Ilan, CURIO, Vantage are all placeholders.

- **Dyson sphere / swarm and the power question.** Not needed by this structure, since no beat depends on it. Your "exponential solar up, exponential power-draw down" reconciliation holds fine as background, and the "normal-looking solar panel as nuclear reactor equivalent" shot survives untouched.

- **Orca mating behaviour.** Doesn't fit this build. The pod is off-screen from B10 to B36, and the material would compete with the matriline motif for the same thematic slot. Park it. It's a strong second-film or series idea.

- **Jonah's fate at B32.** Written as "it costs him," deliberately unresolved. Death is cleaner, survival is warmer. Your call.

- **Does Bali ever come home?** Currently no, and the film is better for it. Worth confirming that's the ending you want before scene work starts.

## Self-Check — `rb.outline`

- **Causality: passes.** Every beat from B4 onward is reachable by *therefore* or *but* from its predecessor, and no beat is entered by information simply appearing. Spot-check on the spine: law forbids chips (B5) → **therefore** she needs an outsider (B6) → **therefore** she has a trail (B10) → **therefore** she is visible to Vantage (B17) → **therefore** the disaster is hers (B26). The order is not interchangeable.

- **Escalation: passes, with one watch item.** Midsection pressure is carried by cost rather than event count. She pays with the rig (B13), with certainty (B15 and B19), with her belief (B22), with her friends' safety (B26). **Watch item:** B16 is the only beat here whose primary job is relief. If the middle sags in outline expansion, B16 is where to add cost, not where to add plot.

- **Closure: passes.** The external goal, get Bali back, is answered, and answered *no*, deliberately. That's stronger than the version where she wins the thing she wanted. The internal goal is answered yes at B30 and B33. The opening image (B1, almost able to speak) and the closing image (B37, understands in real time) are the same measurement taken twice.

- **Fit: passes.** Quest-plus-lie-to-truth is the right family. A pure quest would make act three a rescue and waste the reveal; a pure lie-to-truth would strand the whole middle. Dual-track markers earn their place because the film's two low points are deliberately offset. The feature container is correct: the material has one arc rather than a season's worth, and the midsection has enough independent drive to hold 28 minutes.

- **Hard-fail rules: none triggered.** No interchangeable beats. The middle introduces new pressure at B17, B19, and B24. The ending answers the goal explicitly. Single container, so no arc-budget overdraw. The beat-sheet carrier exposes the engine rather than flattening it.

- **Borderline.** The father's exposition load in B28 and B29 is heavy: two consecutive talk beats at the top of act three. Scene work should distribute it. Some into the lab environment at B23, some into Bali's testimony at B33, so B28 can play as confrontation rather than briefing.
