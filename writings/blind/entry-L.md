# Whales: feature film beat sheet

**What happened:** The notes now support a 115-minute pursuit story that becomes a fight for Bali's right to choose.  

## Story engine

- **Working logline:** After her pod's matriarch vanishes aboard an off-world tanker, a nomadic 15-year-old whale-talker defies her mother and follows the trail across the wormhole network. She discovers that the apparent captor is the father who abandoned her, and that the whale chose to leave.
- **External drive:** Nia must find Bali and bring her home before the tanker's trail goes cold. Each clue narrows the route, but also exposes Nia to the private gate consortium hunting the secret Bali carries.
- **Internal drive:** Nia believes that anyone who leaves without saying goodbye did not love you enough to stay. The rescue mission turns that old wound into certainty: Bali was taken, her father chose his work over her, and her mother would rather surrender than fight.
- **Central dramatic question:** Can Nia defend someone she loves without turning love into another form of captivity?
- **Structure:** A linear quest drives the story. The mystery of who took Bali, why, and what Bali wants keeps changing the meaning of the pursuit. Nia's belief that departure equals abandonment gives way to a harder truth: distance can wound a bond without ending it.
- **Beat carrier:** Each new world gives Nia one useful clue, raises the cost, and corrects one of her assumptions. No stop exists only for spectacle.
- **World:** Earth reaches orbit by a Pacific space elevator and inhabited systems through wormhole gates. Abundant energy and ordinary-looking solar hardware remain in the background. The story does not need a Dyson structure.
- **Hidden work:** Bali and Nia's father have found patterned intelligence in the acoustic distortions of wormholes. Cetaceans can hear and answer it in ways human systems cannot. Their hidden Listening Reef could become humanity's first reciprocal contact with nonhuman life, as well as a consent-based way to navigate the gates.
- **Antagonistic pressure:** A private consortium wants exclusive control of the contact protocol. It once treated cetaceans as biological navigation equipment. Now it follows the search traffic generated by Nia's hacker friend, expecting the children to lead it to Bali and the Reef.
- **Family counterpoint:** Nia's mother protects herself through motion and detachment. Her father protects his work through roots and secrecy. Bali leaves when her matriline is ready to grow without her. Love and fear shape all three choices, and each choice causes real harm. The ending does not excuse their silence. It replaces silence with chosen connection.

## Beat list

1. **The world that listens (minutes 1–6).** In the cold Pacific, Nia swims with Bali's orca pod and tests the translator she has rebuilt since she was eight. It can convert sounds but still stumbles over concepts. Bali corrects Nia by waiting patiently until she listens. We see both Nia's gift and her flaw: she wants uncertainty to become an answer she can act on. *Turn: The opening establishes the bond, the imperfect tool, and Nia's need for certainty.*

2. **A moving home with one fixed wound (minutes 6–12).** Nia returns to a settlement of walking biobotic houses preparing to migrate. Her mother, who designed the houses as a teenager, teaches that healthy systems adapt by moving. A brief reference to Nia's absent father exposes the family's harsher version of that rule: people leave, and you do not chase them. *Turn: Nomadism becomes both a source of wonder and a way to avoid pain.*

3. **Bali is missing (minutes 12–17).** Agitated pod members tell Nia that Bali has been silent for days, beyond the reach of even their long-range calls. Earth outlawed forced tracking after recognizing orca sentience, so Nia cannot locate her mechanically. She recruits her enhanced hacker friend to search orbital imagery instead. *Turn: The search begins, and a law protecting whale autonomy creates its first obstacle.*

4. **The image of a kidnapping (minutes 17–22).** Nia's friend recovers deleted footage of an unmarked ocean tanker descending, enclosing Bali in a flooded hold, and rising toward orbit. The search touches a restricted archive and quietly alerts a consortium watcher. Nia sees only proof that Bali was taken. *Turn: An uncertain clue becomes certainty in Nia's mind, while an unseen pursuer picks up the trail.*

5. **Mother says let her go (minutes 22–27).** Nia demands that the community intervene. Her mother freezes when she sees the tanker's silhouette, then insists there is nothing they can do. “Sometimes people, and whales, just have to go,” she says. Nia answers, “Not without saying goodbye.” Rather than admit that she recognizes a ship connected to Nia's father, her mother confines her. *Turn: Grief becomes open conflict, and adult secrecy closes every sanctioned route to Bali.*

6. **The pod chooses to help (minutes 27–31).** Nia and her friend pack supplies, but the living house will not release them. Nia makes a rough translated plea. In response, the pod sets off a chain of harbor hydrophones while two young orcas tow a supply capsule to the offshore base of the Pacific elevator. The children escape through the house's waterworks and board the rising freight car. Nia leaves the pod a communicator “if they want it” and promises, “We'll find her.” *Turn: Nia begins the journey by making a promise she does not yet understand.*

7. **The chaperone who will not solve anything (minutes 31–36).** Nia's mother sends the community's embodied education ASI to bring the children back. It catches them before orbit, but cannot compel a learner or provide an answer unless death is imminent. Nia argues that returning could leave a sentient person in captivity. The ASI reluctantly becomes their guardian, warning that it will save their lives but not do their homework. *Turn: The children gain adult-scale protection, comic friction, and a witness who keeps testing Nia's reasoning.*

8. **A route through the whale roads (minutes 36–43).** At the orbital colony, the children learn that the tanker scrubbed its gate record. Their search draws the attention of a weathered old spacer who once ferried cetaceans between worlds. He recognizes the ship's seawater-conditioning signature. Ashamed that he worked for the consortium before learning how it used whales, he offers passage to the only two transfer habitats the tanker could have used. *Turn: The pursuit gains a guide and a finite route. The consortium moves from a shadow to a known threat.*

9. **First correction: some whales want the stars (minutes 43–49).** At an exuberant pelagic waystation, traveling dolphins recognize Bali's call sign. They report that she was alert, vocal, and directing the tanker crew toward the next gate. Nia dismisses her behavior as coerced, but the local cetaceans' obvious hunger for travel challenges her belief that leaving home is unnatural. *Turn: The first eyewitness account contradicts Nia's kidnapping story.*

10. **The mistranslation costs them (minutes 49–55).** At an austere waiting ocean, humpbacks refuse off-world passage. Nia decodes Bali's fading message as “held beyond the cold gate” and forces an unauthorized jump. On the other side, she discovers that the disputed sound can mean either “held” or “anchored by choice.” Her bad coordinate strands the ship in a collapsing transit wake. The ASI waits one beat longer than anyone would like before saving them, and their emergency beacon gives the consortium their route. *Turn: Nia's demand for certainty puts everyone in danger and turns a covert pursuit into an active chase.*

11. **Midpoint: the house that put down roots (minutes 55–62).** The surviving fragment leads to a remote ocean world. On its shore, Nia finds one of her mother's walking houses fused into the basalt, its legs grown into a reef. Inside are photographs of Bali, Nia's mother as a young engineer, baby Nia, and the father who left without saying goodbye. He arrives and confirms that he flew the tanker. *Turn: The rescue mystery opens directly into Nia's family wound, and the suspected captor becomes her father.*

12. **A second captivity (minutes 62–68).** Nia's father detects an unknown ship, seals the house, and refuses to tell her where Bali is. He orders the children home for their safety. To Nia, this proves that he controls everyone he claims to protect. She and her friend override the house and flee toward the inland Listening Reef, accidentally pulsing the old network with a signal the consortium can triangulate. *Turn: Father and daughter repeat the same mistake, confusing protection with control, and expose the refuge in the process.*

13. **Bali refuses rescue (minutes 68–74).** Nia reaches the Reef's ocean chamber and opens Bali's enclosure, expecting a reunion. Bali stays. Through images, touch, broken translation, and finally her father's records, Nia learns that Bali contacted him and chose the tanker. Her daughters were ready to lead, her granddaughter was pregnant, and the signal offered work only Bali could do. *Turn: Nia finds Bali but loses the version of success that required bringing her home.*

14. **The truth does not erase the wound (minutes 74–81).** Nia accuses Bali and her father of dressing abandonment up as purpose. Her father explains that any message sent through consortium-owned gates would have exposed the Reef. Then he admits the harder truth: secrecy protected the mission and spared him from facing the people he hurt. Bali believed that a clean departure would stop her pod from following. Now she hears the damage in Nia's message. *Turn: The explanation removes simple villains without removing responsibility. Nia can no longer see either a simple captor or a simple victim.*

15. **What Bali came to answer (minutes 81–87).** Bali sings into the submerged array. An intelligence distributed through the wormhole boundary answers with a pattern that no human or ASI had recognized as thought. The exchange briefly stabilizes a gate without the consortium's control system, and Nia's unfinished translator can bridge the last conceptual gap. Even so, her father asks her to protect the discovery by staying hidden, repeating the bargain that took him from her. *Turn: A family rescue becomes a fight over who has the right to own first contact.*

16. **The hunters arrive (minutes 87–94).** The consortium descends on the exposed Reef with legal claims, containment drones, and a recovery ship built to lift Bali. Nia's father prepares to erase decades of work and move Bali again. Nia tries to drag Bali toward the escape channel. Bali resists them both, and the struggle gives the drones time to clamp the array and isolate her underwater. *Turn: Every attempt to protect Bali through secrecy or possession creates the captivity everyone feared.*

17. **Low point: she led them here (minutes 94–98).** With the Reef failing and their ship pinned, Nia admits that her certainty completed the consortium's trail. The education ASI refuses to tell her what Bali wants because Bali is there to answer for herself. Nia must listen without forcing the translator to decide. At last, she understands Bali's distinction: Bali wants neither escape nor martyrdom. She wants to choose to speak where everyone can hear. *Turn: Nia gives up the rescue fantasy and finds a new objective grounded in consent.*

18. **The open-contact plan (minutes 98–102).** Nia rejects both her father's secrecy and the consortium's ownership. She gives each ally one necessary task. Her friend opens the public gate relays. The old spacer draws off the recovery ship. The ASI protects lives within its emergency rules. Her father keeps the Reef alive. Nia asks her mother for help, not permission, connecting Earth's walking houses and Bali's pod to the broadcast. Her mother chooses trust over retrieval and turns the community into a relay. *Turn: Repairing one relationship creates an option that none of the isolated adults could see.*

19. **Climax: nobody speaks for the whale (minutes 102–110).** As the consortium lifts Bali from the water, Nia enters the flooded array and translates live. She marks which words are hers and which are Bali's. Bali's call crosses the gates. Cetaceans at every transfer habitat answer voluntarily, followed by the wormhole intelligence. The network gives Nia's friend enough distributed control to release the clamps and strand the consortium ship outside the gate lane. When a damaged rig threatens the consortium crew, the ASI supplies the forbidden answer and Nia saves them. She denies the consortium both a monopoly and a martyrdom story. *Turn: Nia wins by making consent visible and the discovery impossible to own, not by forcing Bali home.*

20. **The choice at the waterline (minutes 110–113).** Millions of witnesses now protect the exposed Reef. Bali chooses to remain and lead the next contact. Nia's father chooses to open the work and accept oversight, without asking Nia to forgive him on schedule. Nia gives Bali a communicator paired with the one she left for the pod, now secured by the public relay. They say the goodbye they both tried to avoid. *Turn: Recovery gives way to durable connection, and love survives an honest departure.*

21. **Return song (minutes 113–115).** Nia returns to the migrating houses and tells the whale children, in imperfect orca speech, “Sometimes people have to go. That doesn't mean they're gone. But they still have to say goodbye.” Her mother apologizes without defending herself. Bali's voice arrives through the communicator as the pod gathers around her pregnant granddaughter. The final image holds the sea, a walking home, and the stars in one living network. *Turn: The absence from the opening becomes chosen contact. Motion and roots no longer have to be enemies.*

## Self-check

- **Causality:** Nia's archive search alerts the consortium. Her bad jump exposes the route, her house override reveals the Reef, and her failed rescue allows the consortium to capture Bali. Once Nia accepts responsibility, she can devise the open-contact strategy. The major beats cannot be freely reordered.
- **Escalation:** The pressure grows from a missing family member to an illegal pursuit, a personal betrayal, another apparent abandonment, and the attempted ownership of Bali and first contact. Each world changes what Nia knows or what the search costs.
- **Closure:** Nia finds Bali but does not bring her home. She secures Bali's freedom to choose, restores communication with the pod, confronts both parents' silence, and revises the promise she made when she left.
- **Fit:** The quest supplies forward motion. The mystery changes meaning at the midpoint and again late in the second act. The family arc peaks in the same choice as the external climax. A sequence ladder shows this engine more clearly than a generic 15-point template.
- **Still to solve:** The first half must establish simple visual rules for the wormhole acoustics used in the climax. The sequence outline also needs one memorable human face for the consortium. The father's admission must remain morally costly so the science never excuses his abandonment of Nia.
- **Next step:** Expand the beat sheet into a 35–45-sequence outline and pressure-test the chain from Nia's mistranslation to the Reef's exposure.
