# Beat Sheet: WHALES

*Feature film, approximately 110 pages*

1. **Opening Image (p. 1)**  
Fifteen-year-old Nia swims beneath her community's walking houses with Bali's orca pod. Her translation rig renders Bali's calls in imperfect phrases. Nia feels held by two families and believes that love means staying close.

2. **Theme Stated (p. 5)**  
A young orca strays beyond translator range. Bali says a pod can stretch without breaking, but Nia changes the translation to “family stays together.” It is the belief she must outgrow.

3. **Set-Up (pp. 1–10)**  
Nia divides her time between translating for the pod, working with her gifted techie friend, and helping her mother prepare their biobotic settlement to migrate. Her father is a blank in the family archive. He left without saying goodbye. Then agitated pod members report that Bali has been silent for days.

4. **Catalyst (p. 12)**  
Nia and her friend reconstruct Bali's movements from weather satellites. An unmarked orbital tanker descended over open water and lifted the matriarch alive into its flooded hold.

5. **Debate (pp. 12–25)**  
Nia's mother refuses to chase the tanker. Their people are moving, the pod will move, and “sometimes people, and whales, just have to go.” Nia cannot accept that Bali would leave without saying goodbye. Her mother confines her, so Nia plans an escape while the pod creates a diversion and her friend traces the tanker to a wormhole freight gate.

6. **Break into Two (p. 25)**  
At the space elevator, Nia gives the pod a communicator they can activate “if they want it.” She and her friend board an outbound lift with a promise: “We'll find her.”

7. **B Story (p. 30)**  
The community's embodied education ASI catches them at the orbital port. His rules forbid him from supplying answers unless someone faces imminent death, so he turns their rescue mission into a maddening series of questions. He helps them hire a weathered spacer who knows the cetacean transit worlds and challenges Nia's conviction that withholding an answer is abandonment.

8. **Fun and Games (pp. 30–55)**  
The crew tracks Bali through a lunar tank crowded with thrill-seeking dolphins, a low-gravity humpback sea, and an ice world where whales remember the tanker's call sign. Each clue forces Nia to listen instead of imposing human meanings on whale speech. Corporate recovery craft shadow the search. Without realizing it, Nia is teaching them how to find Bali too.

9. **Midpoint (p. 55)**  
The trail ends at a walking house modeled on her mother's design. Inside, Nia finds photographs of Bali, her mother as a young woman, and the father she barely remembers. His workbench identifies him as the tanker's pilot. The man who left Nia has taken her other mother.

10. **Bad Guys Close In (pp. 55–75)**  
Nia's father returns. He admits that he moved Bali but hides her location from the industrial consortium hunting his research. Furious, Nia steals his maps, locates a secluded inland ocean, and slips past both his safeguards and the approaching recovery craft. Certain that every adult's secrecy is cowardice, she disables the locks on Bali's habitat.

11. **All Is Lost (p. 75)**  
Bali refuses to leave. She summoned Nia's father and chose this place. Her matriline is dividing, and she came here to build an acoustic relay that can carry whale speech through wormholes. To Nia, it feels like a second beloved parent has chosen work over family. Then the consortium arrives, following the trail she exposed.

12. **Dark Night of the Soul (pp. 75–85)**  
With the relay seized, Nia considers taking Bali by force. The ASI replays her mistranslation from the opening: Bali never said family stays together. She said family survives distance. Nia understands that her father's silence caused real harm, but leaving did not end his love. Bali's choice is not Nia's to revoke.

13. **Break into Three (p. 85)**  
Nia joins her father's relay, her friend's network access, and the pod's communicator into a new plan. She will stop hiding the project and let whales across multiple worlds authenticate it publicly, in their own voices.

14. **Finale (pp. 85–105)**  
Nia's friend breaks into the gate controls, the spacer draws off the recovery ships, and the ASI intervenes when the failing habitat turns lethal. Nia and her father repair the relay while Bali assembles a living signal array. The first call crosses the wormhole and reaches Bali's pod. Their public reply exposes the attack and brings nearby ships to the relay's defense. Nia's father stays behind to finish the network, but this time he apologizes for disappearing and schedules their next call.

15. **Final Image (p. 110)**  
Back beneath the walking houses, Nia speaks to the young orcas in halting whale language: “Sometimes people just have to go, but that doesn't mean they're gone.” A juvenile activates the communicator. Bali answers from another world. Nia swims beside her moving home, no longer mistaking distance for loss.

**Structural assessment:** The parallel between Nia's father and Bali gives the midpoint reveal its emotional charge. The wormhole relay also makes the theme the working mechanism of the climax. The weak point is still Bad Guys Close In. The industrial consortium needs a concrete commercial goal and a recognizable human face; without both, its arrival will feel like generic plot pressure rather than a competing worldview.
