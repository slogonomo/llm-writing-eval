# Feature Film Beat Sheet — Nia (Whales)

## Story Engine
- Protagonist: Nia, 15 — nomadic whale-talker who has refined orca translation since she was eight. At home in two families (human and pod), still wounded by the one goodbye she never got.
- Goal (want): Bring matriarch Bali home and force a real farewell. Prove that people and whales do not simply “have to go.”
- Need: Learn that family is who you keep reaching across distance, not who you cage; that leaving can be love with roots; that hurt cuts deepest when it comes from someone you love.
- Central pressure: Every step toward “rescuing” Bali tightens the same wound — abandonment without goodbye — until the captor is revealed as her father, and Bali’s choice mirrors his. Nia must carry a message home in a language she has not mastered.
- Structure family (primary): quest → truth (the outer chase re-aims into lie-to-truth). Secondary: family dual-track of leaving vs. putting down roots.
- Logline lock: After her pod’s matriarch is captured, a nomadic 15-year-old whale-talker defies her mother and journeys off-world in pursuit, only to discover the captor is the father who abandoned her to put down roots.

## Beat List
- Opening imbalance: Pacific Northwest, 2109 — eighty-one years after the Great Awakening. Nia moves underwater with her adopted orca family, fluent enough through lifelong translation work to trade concepts across kinds of mind. Walking biobotic houses, invisible learning surfaces, a community that folds contribution into growth: she is whole in motion. She has never stress-tested the belief that both her families will stay.
- Theme planted: At the house her mother designed young, a neighboring group prepares to leave. Mother: “Sometimes people, and whales, just have to go.” Nia: “Not without saying goodbye.” The line she must re-hear and re-mean is locked. The film names its moral argument before the plot breaks open.
- World / relationship set-up: We meet the enhanced techie friend (outside-community, satellite-capable), the resident embodied ASI (superintelligent comic relief who withholds answers until ignorance would kill), and the pod as family. Forced orca tracking chips have been outlawed since sentience recognition in 2098 — a law that will force human ingenuity when Bali vanishes. Father is absence with no farewell; mother is love and gravity.
- Catalyst: Distressed pod members reach Nia: matriarch Bali has been missing for days — no long-range echolocation. Nia’s scanners find nothing. The adopted family is incomplete; the external story engine starts.
- Debate / early pressure: Friend pulls satellite footage — a tanker scooping Bali from the pod. Nia demands action; mother refuses: they will move on, and so will the whales. Nia gathers supplies; mother finds them and tries to confine her. Want (bring Bali home) and wound (people leave without goodbye) fuse into one irreversible drive.
- Lock-in / Break into Two: The whales assist the escape. At the space elevator (wormhole-era off-world access), Nia gifts the pod a communicator — “if they want it, turn it on” — promises “We’ll find her,” and leaves Earth. Mother’s ASI is already hunting the kids. There is no return to ordinary life without Bali or a new understanding of goodbye.
- B-story crew forms: ASI tags them immediately and must join rather than drag them home — pedagogy under fire. They recruit a swarmy old spacer who knows which worlds can support whales. Chosen family in motion: friend, ASI, spacer. This is the crew that will teach Nia what staying and leaving cost.
- Mid-act pursuit (Fun & Games with cost): Wormhole hops across worlds. Space whales, waiting tanks, cetacean-adapted infrastructure; dolphins travel eagerly, humpbacks mostly stay. Each stop is not tourism: a mistranslation, a burned lead, a local pod’s lore about a mysterious ship and the man who flies it. Nia’s gift thrives under pressure — and her trail, unnoticed, draws industrialist eyes.
- Midpoint turn (false victory → identity reverse): On an unnamed world, a walking house identical to home. Empty. Inside: photos of mother, of Bali, then one of the three of them — Nia at four, mother barely older than Nia is now, and him. Her father. The man who left without goodbye. The trail ends here. The antagonist is no longer a stranger-kidnapper but blood family. Stakes flip from rescuing a whale to confronting abandonment.
- Escalation / Bad Guys Close In: Father arrives (or the house traps them and walks inland toward a fortress while the friend races to override — either path lands them at his work). Resentment; Nia storms. They locate Bali under protection, not a cage. Pursuers close — industrialists who followed the trail Nia blazed. Trust fractures; access narrows. Nia hardens into extraction: take Bali and leave him.
- Crisis / All Is Lost: Confrontation at the whale. Nia has “secured her prize” — and father says: “She wanted to come here. She wants to be part of this.” Bali contacted him. The matriarch chose this still place and this work over the matriline’s wander — the same symmetry as father leaving because the work needed roots and mother needed motion. The adopted mother-figure abandoned the pod the way the blood father abandoned Nia. Nothing clean left to bring home. True hurt only comes from those we love.
- Dark Night: Alone after the reveal, Nia re-hears mother’s line. “Bring Bali home” was her need for a goodbye she never got, not Bali’s need. She also sees what she led to this door: industrialists who would strip the work, the whale, and the project that justified years of silence (and contact blackout). Want and need collide. The external threat pulls the divided family onto one side.
- Break into Three / new plan: Nia chooses neither pure rescue nor pure rejection. She will help father and Bali protect the work and defeat the industrialists — then carry the truth home in a language she has not fully mastered. Family is who we keep reaching, not who we cage.
- Climax: Nia, friend, ASI (finally allowed to stop teaching and start saving), spacer, father, and Bali turn the industrialist assault. The project survives; Bali stays by choice; Nia leaves a communicator so the pod will not be cut off the way she was. Father’s silence is explained by secrecy against those same industrialists — not indifference. External goal re-aimed and achieved; internal need pays off in action.
- Resolution / return: Nia returns to Earth. In the water, she faces the whale-children (and herself) with a touching, jumbled speech: “Sometimes people just have to go — but that doesn’t mean they’re gone. I know you’ll miss her, but soon she’ll be able to reach you.” Theme line inverted and completed.
- Final image / grace note: Same ocean as the opening — the wound named, the lesson lived. Optional: a granddaughter’s pregnancy signals the matriline splitting and renewing, so Bali’s choice aligns with pod evolution rather than pure betrayal. Continuity of reach replaces the fantasy of never leaving.

## Dual-Track Notes (external / internal)
- External: missing matriarch → evidence of capture → off-world pursuit → father’s world → industrialist assault → project protected / communicator left / return.
- Internal: belief that goodbye is owed → defiance of mother’s gravity → rage at father’s face → collapse when Bali’s choice mirrors abandonment → choice to protect rather than reclaim → speech that frees both pod and self.
- Midpoint is where tracks cross: the house photos convert quest into identity crisis. All Is Lost is where internal truth breaks the external plan. Climax is where both tracks co-resolve under one threat.

## Open Decisions Retained from Notes (not locked in beats)
- Space elevator confirmed as primary Earth exit; wormholes as inter-world carrier. Dyson-scale power infrastructure left off-screen unless a shot needs ordinary-looking solar as world texture.
- Father’s scientific work remains directional: life-serving / whalekind / multi-species exploration — pick one before treatment; the beat sheet only requires that it needs stillness and secrecy.
- House-trap alternate vs. father-arrives: either is valid. Trap version raises mid-act set-piece energy; arrival version raises confrontation clarity. Default above allows both landings at the same fortress work.
- Industrialists remain functional antagonists (threat to the still project); specific faction branding deferred.
