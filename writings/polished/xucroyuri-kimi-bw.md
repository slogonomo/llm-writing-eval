# Whales: Feature Film Beat Sheet (xucroyuri-kimi)

Source: John freewrite notes 2026-08-08 + working logline. Route: draft × feature_film × structure → beat_sheet. Structure family: quest/journey with a revelation-reversal midpoint-to-climax spine; the external track (find Bali) and internal track (Nia's wound about abandonment) are braided so each plot turn re-opens the wound. Open questions from the notes are marked [open] where a beat depends on the answer.

## Story Engine

- Protagonist: Nia, 15, nomadic whale-talker of a walking-biobotic-house community in the Pacific Northwest, year 2109. Goal: recover Bali, the orca matriarch who raised her alongside her own mother.
- Opposition: distance and space itself, her mother's refusal, the ASI sent to retrieve her, unknown pursuers, and ultimately the truth: the captor is her father, and Bali left voluntarily.
- Stakes: the pod's matriline and Nia's chosen family; underneath, whether love that leaves is betrayal or necessity.
- Wound: her father left without saying goodbye. Want: bring Bali home. Need: accept that those we love most can leave for reasons that aren't about us, and learn to say the goodbye her father never gave her.
- Thematic engine (from notes): family born vs. family adopted; those who mean the most can hold us back; true hurt only comes from someone we love.

## Beat List

1. **Opening image / status quo.** Pacific Northwest, 2109. Nia, 15, in the water, at home with her adopted orca pod. She's been improving the human-whale translation system since she was 8. World texture: nomadic walking biobotic houses (her mother designed them as a young woman), learning and work fused, intelligence embedded in everything. Establish the pod, the matriarch Bali, and Nia's fluency: partial, assisted, hard-won.

2. **Theme stated (quietly).** Community life: children learning through invisible screens, a window becoming a display of orcas at play. Nia's mother watches her daughter with the whales, warmth with a shadow of worry. The tension between rootedness and movement is planted before it's named.

3. **Inciting incident.** Several pod members approach Nia, disturbed: Bali is missing. Not findable even by long-range echolocation. Gone for days. The pod's distress becomes Nia's problem because she is the only one who can hear it fully.

4. **Investigation / first threshold of effort.** Nia's scanners find nothing; embedded tracking chips were outlawed when orca sentience was recognized. She enlists an outsider friend, an enhanced techie, who pulls satellite footage: a ship descending over the ocean, a large tanker, scooping Bali out of the sea and away from the pod. Kidnapping, confirmed.

5. **Refusal of the call, by the wrong person.** Nia tells her mother: we have to do something. Mother: "There's nothing we can do. We'll be moving on soon, and so will they." Nia: "We can't just let this happen." Mother: "Sometimes people, and whales, just have to go." Nia: "Not without saying goodbye." The mother's line is the thesis Nia will spend the film dismantling, then finally repeat, transformed.

6. **Commitment / confinement.** Nia gathers supplies with the hacker friend. Her mother finds them, tries to stop her, confines her. The person who means most is now literally holding her back: the theme turned into plot.

7. **Escape, assisted by the whales.** The pod helps Nia break out [mechanism open]. Nia and the friend reach the space elevator [open: elevator vs. shipping port; notes lean toward elevator plus colony, easier trail]. She says goodbye to the pod: "Don't worry, we'll find her." She leaves a communicator: "If they want it, turn it on." The goodbye she demanded for others, she gives properly herself. First-act turn: she heads to space.

8. **Pursuit becomes companionship.** Her mother has sent the community's resident embodied ASI to bring the kids back. He finds them quickly and stays. Comic relief with a rule: superintelligent but never allowed to hand over answers [open: general ASI rule vs. education-droid design; the "steps in too late" hijinx variant adds mid-act jeopardy]. The mother's reach follows Nia even into exile.

9. **The guide.** They meet a swarthy old spacer (Jaws-flavored) who knows which worlds can support whales. He'll help them track the ship. Access to the search; also access to space-whale lore.

10. **Fun and games / gathering the trail.** Several worlds, misadventures, local whales, lore about the mysterious ship and the man who flies it. World rule established: space whales are a thing; some go, most don't (humpbacks especially stay; dolphins go everywhere). Human-built infrastructure adapted to whale-friends: waiting tanks, facilities. [open: FTL via wormholes per notes.] Each stop tightens the net on the tanker and paints the pilot as a figure, not yet a person.

11. **Midpoint: the walking house.** A lead on an unnamed world. They land in a region and see something impossible: a walking house, like Nia's home. She's never seen them off Earth. Someone from her community is here. The investigation turns personal in one image.

12. **The revelation.** No one home. They enter. Nia finds pictures: her mother. The matriarch. Old pictures. Baby pictures, and one of the three of them: Nia at four, her mother barely older than Nia is now, and him. Her father. The one who left. The one who didn't say goodbye. The captor is the abandoner; the quest and the wound fuse into one target.

13. **Confrontation, first pass: failure of the old self.** He arrives [alternate per notes: the house traps them and walks them toward an inland fortress; the hacker overrides it in the nick of time. Either version lands the same beat: capture by, or collision with, the father]. Confusion, resentment. Nia storms away. Bad guys close in: the industrialists [placeholder] who have been on her trail. Her quest has led danger to his door.

14. **Climax approach: the prize in hand, the premise inverted.** Nia moves to locate Bali. Big confrontation: she's about to take the whale; the external goal is one action from success. He says: "You don't understand. She wanted to come here. She wants to be part of this." The matriarch contacted him, not the other way around.

15. **The mirror: internal climax.** The truth detonates: this mother, this familial leader, abandoned her family the same way Nia's father abandoned hers. The symmetry hurts. Both parents' choices reframed: he left not to get away from Nia but because the work was here; her mother wouldn't come because her work was there, and she always needed to be moving. His work required sitting still. [open: define the work; one of: helps humanity / helps whalekind / helps all life / discovers alien life / explores uncharted worlds / prevents death. The choice sets the film's final scope.] [open: why no contact; supported by the work's secrecy and the industrialists hunting it.] Nia realizes she may have to go home and carry this message to the pod in a language she has not mastered.

16. **Final confrontation: allegiances rebuilt.** The work is now threatened by the industrialists Nia unwittingly led to him. In a triumphant set piece, Nia, the hacker friend, the ASI, her father, and Bali defeat them, securing the project's next stage. The abandoned daughter becomes the protector of the thing that took her father away; want and need finally align.

17. **Resolution: the goodbye, given correctly.** Nia returns home. To the whale-children she gives a touching, jumbled speech that is also to herself, echoing her mother: "Sometimes people just have to go, but that doesn't mean they're gone. I know you'll miss her, but soon she'll be able to reach you." She gave the matriarch a communicator. The thesis from beat 5 returns, transformed from abandonment into connection across distance.

18. **Closing image.** The pod at sea, the line between matriline and new pod evolution acknowledged [open per notes: Bali's motivation tied to her matriline starting to split; an end-of-film pregnancy for the granddaughter can close with joy]. Water, and this time Nia on the shore side of the glass, a communicator light blinking on.

## Self-Check (against rb.outline)

- **Causality: pass.** Every node has a because: missing matriarch → footage → mother's refusal → confinement → escape → pursuit → guide → trail → walking house → revelation → inversion → industrialist threat → return speech. Nodes are not freely reorderable.
- **Escalation: pass, with one watch item.** Pressure climbs personal (confinement) → spatial (off-world) → relational (the father) → existential (the voluntary-leaving truth) → communal (message to the pod). Watch: the mid-film travelogue (beat 10) is the classic loose-middle risk. Each world stop must add trail information AND sharpen the portrait of the pilot, or it becomes event-filler.
- **Closure: pass.** The ending answers both tracks: externally, Bali is found and the project saved; internally, the mother's beat-5 thesis is returned transformed, and Nia gives the goodbye her father never gave. The communicator device (planted beat 7, echoed beat 15) pays off in the final line.
- **Fit: pass.** Feature-film quest structure suits the material; dual-track rhythm is explicit (search / abandonment wound). No serial-arc budget issues; single film container.
