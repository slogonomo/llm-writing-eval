**Beat Sheet — WHALES (Feature Film, 110 pages)**

**1. Opening Image (p. 1)**
In 2109, fifteen-year-old Nia swims through coastal Pacific waters alongside her adopted orca pod, testing an experimental translation collar. On the shoreline behind her, her mother’s biobotic walking house moves gracefully along the tide line. Nia communicates fluently with Bali, the pod’s elder matriarch, establishing her deep belonging within this hybrid human-cetacean family.

**2. Theme Stated (p. 5)**
While repairing the house’s ambient display shell, Nia’s mother notices Nia’s anxiety about the pod’s migration patterns and tells her: "Sometimes people—and whales—just have to go. You can't force what you love to stay frozen in place." Nia rejects this, believing real family never leaves without a trace.

**3. Set-Up (pp. 1–10)**
We explore Nia’s world: a post-Great Awakening society where education and labor are seamlessly integrated into daily life. Nia has spent seven years refining human-orca translation, filling the void left by her father who vanished years ago without saying goodbye. While her mother embraces a detached, nomadic existence, Nia clings fiercely to the stability of the pod.

**4. Catalyst (p. 12)**
Agitated pod members surround Nia, conveying that matriarch Bali has disappeared from long-range echolocation. Nia enlists her tech-enhanced friend to bypass legal orbital surveillance feeds, uncovering classified footage of an off-world transport ship scooping Bali out of the ocean and breaching Earth's atmosphere.

**5. Debate (pp. 12–25)**
Nia begs her mother to help track the ship, but her mother insists there is nothing they can do and that they must move on. Refusing to accept this, Nia secretly packs supplies with her friend. Her mother catches them, confining Nia inside the automated walking house to prevent her from pursuing a suicide mission into space.

**6. Break into Two (p. 25)**
The young orcas assist Nia by manipulating the walking house's submerged coastal sensors, triggering an emergency override that unlocks the doors. Nia gives the pod a long-range sub-space communicator, promises to bring Bali home, and sneaks aboard a space elevator bound for the orbital transit hubs.

**7. B Story (p. 30)**
Nia’s mother sends their resident embodied superintelligent education ASI—a witty, rule-bound droid who refuses to give direct answers—to retrieve the teenagers. Instead of forcing them home, the ASI agrees to chaperone them alongside a crusty, veteran spacer who knows off-world cetacean migration routes, forcing Nia to learn how to lead and make decisions without adult hand-holding.

**8. Fun and Games (pp. 30–55)**
Nia, her friend, the ASI, and the spacer navigate wormhole jump stations and water-covered moons, consulting off-world dolphin colonies to trace the mysterious ship. Nia uses her translation rig in strange alien biomes, proving her skills, while an unseen corporate industrialist syndicate silently tracks her digital breadcrumbs across the galaxy.

**9. Midpoint (p. 55)**
A false victory: Nia tracks the vessel to a remote sanctuary world and discovers a biobotic walking house identical to her mother's design. Breaking inside to free Bali, Nia finds old holos of her mother, Bali, and her missing father—realizing the "captor" she has been hunting is the father who abandoned her, and that Bali came willingly to assist his research.

**10. Bad Guys Close In (pp. 55–75)**
Nia’s father confronts her, revealing he left not out of coldness, but because his stationary research into inter-species consciousness required roots her nomadic mother refused to plant. The emotional shock deepens when the industrialist syndicate—having followed Nia’s ship—lands heavily armed extraction teams to seize the sanctuary’s breakthrough tech and harvest Bali for corporate exploitation.

**11. All Is Lost (p. 75)**
The industrialists breach the compound, seizing the research core and locking Bali into a heavy transport container. Nia’s father is injured, her friend’s tech is fried, and Nia realizes her reckless drive to "rescue" a whale who didn't want rescuing has exposed both her father and Bali to total destruction. She has lost everything she set out to protect.

**12. Dark Night of the Soul (pp. 75–85)**
Sheltering in the ruined sanctuary, Nia talks openly with her wounded father and the ASI. She finally comprehends the truth of her mother’s words: loving someone means respecting their autonomy to leave, and distance does not sever emotional bonds. Her wound begins to heal as she forgives her father and accepts that family isn't about control, but connection.

**13. Break into Three (p. 85)**
Nia remembers the long-range communicator she left with the Earth pod and realizes she can use the sanctuary's planetary solar array to boost her translation rig. With the ASI agreeing to bypass his safety parameters to assist her, Nia rallies her father, her friend, and local space-dolphins to launch a high-stakes counter-interception before the industrialists reach the orbital jump gate.

**14. Finale (pp. 85–105)**
Nia coordinates a multi-front assault on the industrialist flagship. Using her translation rig, she commands space-cetaceans to disrupt the freighter’s gravity drives while her friend overrides the security doors. Nia personally confronts the corporate commander, disabling the transport containment and freeing Bali. Together with her father and the pod, she repels the corporate fleet and secures the sanctuary.

**15. Final Image (p. 110)**
Back on Earth, Nia swims once more with her pod while her mother's walking house rests on the beach. Nia activates the interstellar array, establishing a permanent two-way audio bridge between the Earth pod and Bali across the stars. Standing beside her mother, Nia speaks to the young orcas with quiet maturity: "Sometimes people just have to go... but that doesn't mean they're gone."

---

**Structural Assessment:** The 15-beat structure effectively transforms a personal rescue mission into a resonant coming-of-age story, anchored by strong emotional turning points at the Midpoint and All Is Lost beats. The single most vulnerable beat is the Break into Two (p. 25); relying on the Earth pod to trigger the walking house's emergency override feels slightly convenient unless Nia’s translation collar and a specific, pre-established behavioral trigger are clearly dramatized as an intentional collaboration rather than a lucky fluke.
