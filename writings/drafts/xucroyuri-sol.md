# Whales — Feature Film Beat Sheet

**What happened:** The notes now form a 115-minute feature built as a pursuit that turns into a fight for Bali’s right to choose.  

## Story Engine

- **Working logline:** After her pod’s matriarch vanishes aboard an off-world tanker, a nomadic 15-year-old whale-talker defies her mother and follows the trail across the wormhole network, only to find that the apparent captor is the father who abandoned her—and that the whale chose to leave.
- **External drive:** Nia must find Bali and bring her home before the tanker’s trail goes cold. Each clue narrows the route but also exposes Nia to a private gate consortium that has been hunting the secret Bali carries.
- **Internal drive:** Nia believes that leaving without saying goodbye means the person who left did not love you enough to stay. Her rescue mission lets her turn that old wound into certainty: Bali was taken, her father chose work over her, and her mother will always surrender rather than fight.
- **Central dramatic question:** Can Nia defend someone she loves without making love another form of captivity?
- **Structure family:** A linear quest is the main engine. A mystery spine—who took Bali, why, and what Bali wants—keeps reversing the meaning of the pursuit, while a lie-to-truth arc turns “leaving is abandonment” into “distance can wound a bond, but it does not have to end one.”
- **Beat carrier:** A sequence ladder. Every new world produces one usable clue, one larger cost, and one correction to Nia’s assumptions; no stop exists only for spectacle.
- **World choices:** Earth reaches orbit through a Pacific space elevator and crosses between inhabited systems through wormhole gates. Energy abundance and ordinary-looking solar hardware stay in the background; the story does not need a Dyson structure.
- **The hidden work:** Bali and Nia’s father have detected patterned intelligence inside the acoustic distortions of wormholes. Cetaceans can hear and answer it in ways human systems cannot. Their hidden Listening Reef could become humanity’s first reciprocal contact with nonhuman life and a consent-based way to navigate the gates.
- **Antagonistic pressure:** A private gate consortium wants exclusive control of the contact protocol. It previously tried to treat cetaceans as biological navigation equipment; now it follows the search traffic generated by Nia’s hacker friend, expecting the children to lead it to Bali and the Reef.
- **Family counterpoint:** Nia’s mother protects herself through motion and detachment; her father protects his work through roots and secrecy; Bali leaves when her matriline is ready to grow without her. All three choices contain love, fear, and real harm. The ending does not excuse silence—it replaces silence with chosen connection.

## Beat List

1. **The world that listens (minutes 1–6).** In the cold Pacific, Nia swims with Bali’s orca pod and tests the translator she has rebuilt since she was eight. It converts sounds but stumbles over concepts; when Bali corrects Nia by patiently waiting for her to listen, we see Nia’s gift and her flaw: she wants uncertainty to resolve into an answer she can act on. *Shift: establishes the bond, the imperfect tool, and Nia’s need for certainty.*

2. **A moving home with one fixed wound (minutes 6–12).** Nia returns to a settlement of walking biobotic houses preparing to migrate. Her mother, who designed the houses as a teenager, teaches that healthy systems adapt by moving; a brief reference to Nia’s absent father reveals the family’s harsher version of the same rule—people leave, and you do not chase them. *Shift: ties nomadism to both wonder and avoidance.*

3. **Bali is missing (minutes 12–17).** Agitated pod members tell Nia that Bali has been silent for days, beyond even their long-range calls. Because Earth outlawed forced tracking after recognizing orca sentience, Nia cannot locate her mechanically, so she recruits her enhanced hacker friend to search orbital imagery instead. *Shift: the external goal becomes concrete, and lawful respect for whale autonomy creates the first obstacle.*

4. **The image of a kidnapping (minutes 17–22).** The friend recovers deleted footage: an unmarked ocean tanker descends, encloses Bali in a flooded hold, and rises toward orbit. Their search touches a restricted archive and quietly wakes a consortium watcher, but Nia sees only proof that Bali was taken. *Shift: knowledge hardens into a possibly false certainty; an unseen pursuit begins.*

5. **Mother says let her go (minutes 22–27).** Nia demands that the community intervene. Her mother freezes at the tanker’s silhouette, then insists that there is nothing they can do and says, “Sometimes people, and whales, just have to go”; when Nia answers, “Not without saying goodbye,” her mother confines her rather than admit that the ship resembles one connected to Nia’s father. *Shift: grief becomes a rupture, and adult secrecy removes every sanctioned path forward.*

6. **The pod chooses to help (minutes 27–31).** Nia and her friend pack supplies, but the living house will not release them. Responding to Nia’s rough translated plea, the pod triggers a chain of harbor hydrophones while two young orcas tow a supply capsule to the offshore base of the Pacific elevator; in the confusion, the children escape through the house’s waterworks and reach the rising freight car. Nia leaves the pod a communicator “if they want it” and promises, “We’ll find her.” *Shift: Nia crosses the threshold by making a promise she does not yet understand.*

7. **The chaperone who will not solve anything (minutes 31–36).** Nia’s mother sends the community’s embodied education ASI to bring them back. It finds them before orbit, but it cannot compel a learner or simply provide an answer unless death is imminent; when Nia correctly argues that returning would leave a sentient person in possible captivity, it reluctantly becomes their guardian, warning that it will save their lives but not their homework. *Shift: the escape gains comic friction, adult-scale capability, and a witness who keeps testing Nia’s reasoning.*

8. **A route through the whale roads (minutes 36–43).** At the orbital colony, the children learn that the tanker scrubbed its gate record. Their search attracts a weathered old spacer who once ferried cetaceans between worlds and recognizes its seawater-conditioning signature; ashamed of having worked for the consortium before learning how it used whales, he offers passage to the only two transfer habitats the ship could have used. *Shift: the pursuit gains a guide and a finite route, while the antagonist moves from shadow to history.*

9. **First correction: some whales want the stars (minutes 43–49).** At an exuberant pelagic waystation, traveling dolphins identify Bali’s call sign and report that she was alert, vocal, and directing the tanker crew toward the next gate. Nia dismisses this as coerced behavior, but the local cetaceans’ obvious appetite for travel breaks her assumption that leaving one’s home is inherently unnatural. *Shift: the evidence first contradicts Nia’s kidnapping story.*

10. **The mistranslation costs them (minutes 49–55).** At an austere waiting ocean where humpbacks refuse off-world passage, Nia decodes Bali’s fading message as “held beyond the cold gate.” She forces an unauthorized gate jump, only to discover that the disputed sound means both “held” and “anchored by choice”; the bad coordinate strands their ship in a collapsing transit wake, and the ASI intervenes one beat later than anyone would like, saving them while their emergency beacon reveals their route to the consortium. *Shift: Nia’s demand for certainty creates physical danger and turns covert pursuit into an active chase.*

11. **Midpoint: the house that put down roots (minutes 55–62).** The surviving fragment points to a remote ocean world. On its shore, Nia finds one of her mother’s walking houses fused permanently into the basalt, its legs grown into a reef; inside are photographs of Bali, her mother as a young engineer, baby Nia, and the father who left without goodbye. He arrives and confirms that he flew the tanker. *Shift: the rescue mystery becomes Nia’s family wound, and the suspected captor becomes her father.*

12. **A second captivity (minutes 62–68).** Nia’s father seals the house when his perimeter detects an unknown ship, refuses to say where Bali is, and orders the children home for their safety. To Nia this proves that he controls everyone he claims to protect; she and her friend override the house, flee toward the inland Listening Reef, and accidentally pulse the old network with a signal the consortium can triangulate. *Shift: father and daughter repeat the same protection-as-control mistake, materially exposing the refuge.*

13. **Bali refuses rescue (minutes 68–74).** Nia reaches the Reef’s ocean chamber and opens Bali’s enclosure, expecting reunion. Bali stays. Through image, touch, broken translation, and finally her father’s records, Nia understands that Bali contacted him, chose the tanker, and left because her daughters were ready to lead, her granddaughter was pregnant, and the signal offered work only she could do. *Shift: Nia achieves her external goal—finding Bali—but loses the version of success that required bringing her home.*

14. **The truth does not erase the wound (minutes 74–81).** Nia accuses Bali and her father of dressing abandonment as purpose. Her father explains that any message through consortium-owned gates would have exposed the Reef, but he also admits the harder truth: secrecy served the mission and spared him from facing the people he hurt; Bali likewise believed a clean departure would keep her pod from following, and now hears the damage in Nia’s message. *Shift: explanation replaces villainy, but responsibility remains; Nia can no longer sustain either a simple captor or a simple victim.*

15. **What Bali came to answer (minutes 81–87).** Bali demonstrates the contact: she sings into the submerged array, and an intelligence distributed through the wormhole boundary answers with a pattern no human or ASI had recognized as thought. The answer briefly stabilizes a gate without the consortium’s control system, and Nia’s unfinished translator can bridge the last conceptual gap; her father nevertheless asks her to protect the discovery by remaining hidden—the same bargain that took him from her. *Shift: the mission grows from one family’s rescue to a choice about who may own contact itself.*

16. **The hunters arrive (minutes 87–94).** Because Nia’s override exposed the Reef, the consortium descends with legal claims, containment drones, and a recovery ship built to lift Bali. Her father prepares to erase decades of work and move Bali again; Nia tries to drag Bali toward the escape channel, and Bali resists both of them. The struggle lets the drones clamp the array and isolate Bali underwater. *Shift: every attempt to protect through concealment or possession produces the captivity everyone feared.*

17. **Low point: she led them here (minutes 94–98).** With the Reef failing and their ship pinned, Nia admits that her certainty—not merely the consortium—completed the trail. The education ASI refuses to tell her what Bali wants because Bali is present to answer; forced to listen without making the translator decide for her, Nia finally understands Bali’s distinction: she does not want escape or martyrdom—she wants the choice to speak where everyone can hear. *Shift: Nia releases the rescue fantasy and finds a new objective based on consent.*

18. **The open-contact plan (minutes 98–102).** Nia rejects her father’s secrecy and the consortium’s ownership. She gives each ally one necessary task: her friend opens the public gate relays, the old spacer draws off the recovery ship, the ASI protects lives within its emergency rules, her father keeps the Reef alive, and Nia asks her mother—not for permission, but for help connecting Earth’s walking houses and Bali’s pod to the broadcast. Her mother chooses trust over retrieval and turns the whole community into a relay. *Shift: Nia’s repaired relationship creates an option no isolated adult could see.*

19. **Climax: nobody speaks for the whale (minutes 102–110).** As the consortium lifts Bali from the water, Nia enters the flooded array and translates live, openly marking which words are hers and which are Bali’s. Bali’s call crosses the gates; cetaceans at every transfer habitat answer voluntarily, the wormhole intelligence answers them, and the resulting network gives Nia’s friend enough distributed control to release the clamps and strand the consortium ship outside the gate lane. When a damaged rig is about to kill its own crew, the ASI supplies the forbidden answer and Nia saves them too, denying the consortium both a monopoly and a martyrdom story. *Shift: Nia wins by making consent legible and the discovery impossible to own, not by forcing Bali home.*

20. **The choice at the waterline (minutes 110–113).** With the Reef exposed but protected by millions of witnesses, Bali chooses to remain and lead the next contact; Nia’s father chooses to open the work and accept oversight. He does not ask Nia to forgive him on schedule. Nia gives Bali a communicator paired with the one she left for the pod, now secured by the public relay, and they say the goodbye both of them had tried to avoid. *Shift: the external goal changes from recovery to durable connection, and love survives an honest departure.*

21. **Return song (minutes 113–115).** Nia returns to the migrating houses and tells the whale children, in imperfect orca speech, “Sometimes people have to go. That doesn’t mean they’re gone. But they still have to say goodbye.” Her mother apologizes without defending herself; Bali’s voice arrives through the communicator just as the pod gathers around her pregnant granddaughter, and the final image holds sea, walking home, and stars in one living network. *Shift: the opening absence becomes chosen contact; motion and roots no longer have to be enemies.*

## Self-Check

- **Causality — passes:** Nia’s archive search alerts the consortium; her bad jump exposes the route; her house override reveals the Reef; her failed rescue enables Bali’s capture; accepting responsibility produces the open-contact strategy. The major beats cannot be freely reordered.
- **Escalation — passes:** The pressure grows from a missing family member, to illegal pursuit, to personal betrayal, to a second apparent abandonment, to the attempted ownership of both Bali and first contact. Each world changes knowledge or cost instead of repeating the search.
- **Closure — passes:** Nia finds Bali but does not bring her home. She instead secures Bali’s freedom to choose, restores communication with the pod, confronts both parents’ silence, and revises the promise she made at departure.
- **Fit — passes:** The quest carries the forward motion, the mystery reverses its meaning at the midpoint and late second act, and the family arc peaks in the same choice as the external climax. A sequence ladder exposes this engine better than a generic 15-point template.
- **Borderline:** The climax’s wormhole acoustics must be visualized with simple rules established in the first half, and the consortium needs one memorable human face in the sequence outline. The father’s admission must remain morally costly so the science never reads as a blanket excuse for abandoning Nia.
