# Beat Sheet — WHALES

*Feature film, approximately 110 pages*

1. **Opening Image (p. 1)**  
Nia, fifteen, swims beneath her community's walking houses with Bali's orca pod. Her translation rig renders Bali's calls as imperfect phrases, but Nia feels held by two families and believes love means staying close.

2. **Theme Stated (p. 5)**  
When a young orca wanders beyond translator range, Bali says a pod can stretch without breaking. Nia revises the translation to “family stays together,” revealing the belief she must outgrow.

3. **Set-Up (pp. 1–10)**  
Nia moves between translation work, her gifted techie friend, and a mother preparing their biobotic settlement to migrate. Her father is a blank in the family archive: he left without goodbye. Then agitated pod members report that Bali has been silent for days.

4. **Catalyst (p. 12)**  
Nia and her friend reconstruct Bali's movements from weather satellites. They find an unmarked orbital tanker descending over open water and lifting the matriarch alive into a flooded hold.

5. **Debate (pp. 12–25)**  
Nia's mother refuses pursuit: their people are moving, the pod will move, and “sometimes people, and whales, just have to go.” Nia insists Bali did not say goodbye. Confined by her mother, she plots escape as the pod creates a diversion and her friend traces the tanker to a wormhole freight gate.

6. **Break into Two (p. 25)**  
Nia reaches the space elevator and gives the pod a communicator they may activate “if they want it.” She and her friend board an outbound lift, promising, “We'll find her.”

7. **B Story (p. 30)**  
The community's embodied education ASI catches them at the orbital port but, forbidden to supply answers unless death is imminent, turns their retrieval into maddening questions. He helps them hire a weathered spacer who knows the cetacean transit worlds and challenges Nia's belief that withholding an answer equals abandonment.

8. **Fun and Games (pp. 30–55)**  
The crew follows Bali through a lunar tank packed with thrill-seeking dolphins, a low-gravity humpback sea, and an ice world whose whales remember the tanker's call sign. Nia wins each clue by listening rather than forcing human meanings onto whale speech. Corporate recovery craft shadow the search; unknowingly, Nia is teaching them how to find Bali too.

9. **Midpoint (p. 55)**  
At the destination, Nia finds a walking house modeled on her mother's design. Inside are photographs of Bali, Nia's young mother, and the father Nia barely remembers; his workbench identifies him as the tanker's pilot. The man who left Nia has taken her other mother.

10. **Bad Guys Close In (pp. 55–75)**  
Nia's father returns and admits moving Bali but conceals her location from the industrial consortium tracking his research. Nia storms out, uses his maps to find a secluded inland ocean, and evades both his safeguards and approaching recovery craft. She disables Bali's habitat locks, convinced every adult's secrecy was cowardice.

11. **All Is Lost (p. 75)**  
Bali refuses to leave. She summoned Nia's father and chose this place: her matriline is dividing, and she came to build an acoustic relay carrying whale speech through wormholes. Nia hears a second beloved parent choosing work over family as the consortium arrives on the trail she exposed.

12. **Dark Night of the Soul (pp. 75–85)**  
With the relay seized, Nia considers taking Bali by force. The ASI replays her opening mistranslation: Bali never said family stays together; she said family survives distance. Nia sees that her father's silence caused real harm, but leaving did not end his love—and Bali's choice is not hers to revoke.

13. **Break into Three (p. 85)**  
Nia combines her father's relay, her friend's network access, and the pod's communicator. Instead of hiding the project, she will let whales across multiple worlds authenticate it publicly in their own voices.

14. **Finale (pp. 85–105)**  
Her friend infiltrates the gate controls, the spacer draws off recovery ships, and the ASI intervenes when the failing habitat becomes lethal. Nia and her father repair the relay while Bali organizes a living signal array. The first wormhole-spanning call reaches Bali's pod; its public reply exposes the attack and rallies nearby ships. Nia's father stays to finish the network, but apologizes for disappearing and schedules their next call.

15. **Final Image (p. 110)**  
Back beneath the walking houses, Nia tells the young orcas in halting whale speech: “Sometimes people just have to go, but that doesn't mean they're gone.” A juvenile activates the communicator, Bali answers from another world, and Nia swims beside her moving home without mistaking distance for loss.

**Structural Assessment:** The father/Bali symmetry gives the reveal emotional force, while the wormhole relay turns the theme into the physical mechanism of the climax. The most vulnerable beat is Bad Guys Close In: the industrial consortium still needs a specific commercial goal and a recognizable human face, or its arrival will feel like plot pressure rather than the consequence of a competing worldview.
