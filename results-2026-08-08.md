## The two instruments measure different things

The panel converged on design: plants and payoffs, causality, an antagonist with a motive, a climax the story must arrive at. H won on exactly that (the unsolved whale-signal class paid off as the climax skill; "her love is the tracking device"; the mother's houses finally staying). You rewarded voice, momentum, and ideas worth stealing, and penalized boredom and AI-feel. The three biggest splits are the proof:

- **B (your 9.5, their 11th).** Every judge called its third act generic action. Your own note already contained the reconciliation: not the sheet you'd use, but a writer you'd give act passes to. The panel scored the sheet; you partly scored the writer.
- **L (your 7, their 2nd).** Five judges praised its consent architecture ("nobody speaks for the whale") and the mistranslation that costs the crew a jump. You found Act Three long-winded. Both true; it is the densest entry.
- **M (your 9, their 13th).** All six flagged the structural looseness (Bali found at the Midpoint, the reveal crowded into All Is Lost). But Opus wrote "the only entry with a surprise in it... That instinct is worth more than the draft around it," DeepSeek scored it 8 and called it "the most original voice" with "the most memorable image of the batch" (the beach goodbye), and Sol called the roots ending fresh. Nobody said "human." They read it as the set's most original underdeveloped draft — which is what a writer's own fast pass is.

## Method

Thirteen entries read blind (entry files only), one judge per model family on the same lanes and models that wrote, scored 1–10 on: is it interesting, does it make you want to know more, does it have a story. Judges did not know one entry was human.

## Addendum (later 8/8): John's verdicts and a method correction

**On the steal list, John's calls:** whale cognition as the wormhole-navigation stake — keep, "one of the best ideas for the research." The mother's arrival beat in H — despised (the she-vanishes critique stands; that fix doesn't). The consent arc and most of the dialogue cited above (including the mistranslation lines as written) — hated. Treat the steal list as pruned to: the H navigation stake and signal-class machinery, B's set-piece energy, M's roots ending and beach goodbye.

**Method correction (his critique, accepted):** the round-1 judge prompt asked for "story development work... Is it interesting? Does it make you want to know more? Does it have a story?" — criteria that reward development volume and read compression as thinness. The 15-beat entries were doing a different job: boil the story to its 15 essential beats in the skill's format (the skill's own output contract says 600–900 words; every entry blew through it — see word counts in round 2). Judging the two formats against each other on those criteria favored the long sheets structurally. His line for the record: "To score writing without scoring the writer is like scoring music without listening... Writing is for the purpose of communicating thoughts and feelings; it is not for the purpose of generating text that fits a format."

**Round 2 (running):** the seven 15-beat entries only (A, B, C, G, I, J, M), same six judges blind, three separate 1–10 scores each — skill-fit (judges get the skill file, nothing pointed out about the example's shape), emotive quality, and originality. Open questions it tests: do any judges notice the entries dwarf the skill's own example unprompted; does the volume bias survive when skill-fit is scored on its own axis.

Skill's own contract: 600–900 words (its example: 774). Axis winners: skill-fit C and I tied (J third by 0.09); emotive I; creativity J.

**Did anyone notice the example's shape unprompted? One of six.** Sol audited length against the contract on every entry — "runs past the 900-word limit" (A), "at 1,266 words it substantially exceeds the brief" (C), "1,424 words... depart sharply from the required 600–900-word sheet" (I), "At 882 words it follows the requested form exactly" (J, its skill-fit 10), "stays within the word limit" (M). DeepSeek noticed only I. Opus never mentioned length but was the only judge to catch the skill's banned-vocabulary rule ("structural vocabulary the skill explicitly bans" on A). **And the sharpest finding: two judges cited length only to penalize M — and were factually wrong both times.** Kimi: "Roughly half the target length." Gemini: "falling short of the required word count." M is 778 words, inside the band, two words from the example. The band-compliant sheet read as thin next to its overweight siblings, so judges reverse-engineered a rule violation to justify the feeling. Disclosure: the one judge who enforced the length contract (Sol) is also the author of the only compliant model entry (J); principled or convenient, the numbers can't say.

**Volume bias: confirmed, but not where John predicted.** Length correlates r≈0.51 with creativity (0.34 excluding M) — his predicted axis is actually the least biased, and its winner J is the second-shortest sheet; the mistranslation device beat volume. The bias hides upstream: r≈0.79 between length and *emotive quality*, and r≈0.60 with *skill-fit* — the axis where exceeding 900 words is literally a contract violation. Thoroughness read as compliance even when it was the opposite. (Seven data points; directional, not proof.)

**Peer-norming (new failure mode for the eval file):** several judges scored M's beat mapping as error — Midpoint "pure celebration, no reversal," reveal "delayed" to All Is Lost — but a false-victory Midpoint is canonical Save the Cat, and reveal-at-All-Is-Lost is a defensible alternative mapping. The other six sheets all put the reveal at the Midpoint, and deviation from the sibling consensus got scored as deviation from the skill. Blind sets drawn from one premise norm judges on the peer group, not the spec.

**Self-preference, skill-fit axis:** Sol +1.4 and Opus +1.3 on their own entries versus the other judges' mean; everyone else within ±0.6.

**M's report card:** last on skill-fit (5.17, two factually false dings included), last on emotive (6.00 — though Opus called the beach scene "the warmest human moment in the panel"), tied-last with B on creativity at 6.67 but with the widest spread (4 from Gemini, 8s from Sol and DeepSeek) — and the roots ending was again singled out across ballots as the set's freshest Final Image.

**Method verdict:** axis-splitting worked — the three rank orders differ substantially, which is exactly the structure round 1's blended number was hiding. Handing judges the spec only changed the judge that actually used it. B's fall (9.5 from John, bottom of two panel axes) says the John-panel split survives better rubrics: it is a real difference in what is being valued, not a prompt artifact.
