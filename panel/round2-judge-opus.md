# Round 2 panel scores — judge: Opus

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**What happened:** I read the beat-sheet-builder skill and the seven 15-beat entries blind, and scored each on three axes: skill compliance, emotive quality, and creativity.

**Date:** 2026-08-08

Scale is 1–10 on each axis, one sentence of reasoning each. Entries appear in the order I was given them.

---

## Entry A

**Skill compliance: 6** — All 15 beats are present at the right page marks, but the writing leans on the structural vocabulary the skill explicitly bans ("Want and wound lock," "False victory: the trail ends here"), and Bad Guys Close In hedges between two versions of the scene in a parenthetical instead of committing to one concrete event.

**Emotive quality: 7** — The compression produces a few genuinely hard lines ("Nothing clean left to bring home," "Nia's adopted mother-figure left the pod the way her blood father left her"), but the telegraphic register keeps most beats at the level of summary rather than scene.

**Creativity: 7** — The strongest original move is naming the two departures as the same act — the father left because the work needed roots, Bali left for the same reason — and the pregnancy grace note that splits and renews the matriline is a real idea, not decoration.

## Entry B

**Skill compliance: 9** — Every beat is a specific, staged event with correct markers, the internal arc is visible at Midpoint, All Is Lost, and Final Image, and the assessment names one weakest beat with a concrete reason and a concrete fix.

**Emotive quality: 6** — Clear and readable, but the prose is functional throughout, and the third act converts a story about abandonment into hardware — mercenaries, haul tanks, combat subroutines — which crowds out the feeling the ending needs.

**Creativity: 6** — The invented mechanics are sound (telemetry trail, flood override, the syndicate wanting Bali for bio-processing) but conventional; this is the entry that resolves the premise most like a standard action film.

## Entry C

**Skill compliance: 9** — Header restates logline, want, need, and antagonist; all 15 beats are concrete and correctly placed; the assessment names Break into Three as the weakest beat, explains exactly why it is unearned, and flags the open premise questions the skill asks the writer to surface.

**Emotive quality: 9** — The beats are written as felt experience rather than plot summary — "the symmetry is unbearable," "she thinks of the communicator she gave the pod: *if they want it*" — and the Dark Night turns the mother's line over in a way that lands as a genuine reversal of meaning.

**Creativity: 7** — The reframe of "sometimes people just have to go" from surrender into love-that-doesn't-chain is well earned, and "a language the attackers can't hear" is a good finale idea, though the third act stays more asserted than invented.

## Entry G

**Skill compliance: 8** — Beats and markers are correct and each one is a real event, but Set-Up already delivers the news that Bali has gone silent, which blunts the Catalyst two beats later.

**Emotive quality: 8** — Restrained and confident; the Midpoint reveal ("the captor and the wound are the same person") and the final communicator click do their work without pushing, though a few beats stay at reported distance.

**Creativity: 7** — The world-hopping is the most specific of any entry — a lunar tank of dolphins who love travel, a low-gravity humpback sea whose residents refuse to move, an ice world remembering a call sign — and each stop dramatizes listening rather than translating.

## Entry I

**Skill compliance: 10** — This is the only entry that completes the full skill contract: logline, want, need, a three-stage antagonist, 15 concrete beats at correct marks, a structural assessment naming both the weakest beat and a second real problem (the mother vanishes for 85 pages), and an explicit section listing the beats where the source premise is too thin to write specifically.

**Emotive quality: 9** — The best sentence-level writing in the set — "echolocating a hole in the world," "Home is always leaving," "the translator gives Nia the word for *missing*, then a word it cannot render at all" — and the Dark Night reading of the mother's resignation as "the only sentence left to the one who wouldn't stay" reframes a character in one line.

**Creativity: 8** — Replacing the given theme line with "Everything that matters can walk" / "Not everything wants to" states both sides of the argument as an argument, and the closing image of the mother staying put a moment longer than the house wants her to is the sharpest small invention in the panel.

## Entry J

**Skill compliance: 9** — All beats present, concrete, correctly marked, with the internal arc visible at every required station and a specific weakest-beat call on the consortium's missing face and goal.

**Emotive quality: 7** — Efficient and clean rather than moving; the Dark Night reveal is intellectually satisfying and the final "no longer mistaking distance for loss" is well judged, but most beats prioritize mechanism over feeling.

**Creativity: 9** — The mistranslation device is the best structural invention in the panel: at Theme Stated, Bali says a pod can stretch without breaking and Nia writes down "family stays together," and the ASI replays that mistranslation in the Dark Night to break her — the protagonist's flaw is literally encoded in her own tool. The acoustic relay that carries whale speech through wormholes also answers the open question of what the father's work is, and makes the theme the machine that wins the climax.

## Entry M

**Skill compliance: 4** — The beat map is misassigned: the Midpoint is a celebration with no reversal, the walking-house discovery is filed under All Is Lost at pp. 65–75, the confrontation is filed under Dark Night, there is no low point anywhere, Bad Guys Close In runs 5 pages instead of 20, and Fun and Games is exactly the placeholder the skill forbids ("Each planet is an adventure").

**Emotive quality: 6** — Mostly plain summary, but it contains the warmest human moment in the panel — Cal on the beach, "I guess this is goodbye," and Nia's "It isn't" — which is a real scene where other entries give a voiceover.

**Creativity: 7** — Two genuine inventions: the pursuers turn out to be hunting the father rather than working for him, and the ending inverts the whole premise by having the nomad choose to stay and put down roots, which is the only entry where the protagonist's answer costs her something of her own.
