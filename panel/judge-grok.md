## Scores (entry order)

### A — 8/10
Clean Save-the-Cat spine with real bookends: theme line, father Midpoint, and All Is Lost as chosen abandonment rather than a capture fail. Fun and Games still risks tourism, but the want/need lock and the industrialist irony land enough story that I want the outline next.

### B — 7/10
Has a complete movie-shaped chase and a punchier action finale, but packs father reveal and Bali’s choice too close at the Midpoint and rushes the Dark Night into first aid. Interesting set pieces; less emotional torque than the stronger sheets.

### C — 8/10
Logline, want/need, and staged antagonists are locked cleanly; Midpoint and All Is Lost are properly separated escalations. Same solid architecture as the best template fills, with an honest flag that Act Three still needs a planted capability rather than sentiment.

### D — 8/10
Strong as development work: dual-track engine, explicit therefore-links, and open questions left open instead of papered over. The chase-to-belief-collapse story is clear; the multi-world middle is still the sag risk, but the self-check makes me trust the next pass.

### E — 8/10
Complete dual-track beat list with costs named in the mid-act pursuit and both house-trap and father-arrives kept as valid landings. Story is all there; less distinctive voice and fewer load-bearing inventions than the top tier.

### F — 9/10
Best “engine on the page” among the mid-long sheets: external goal, internal cost, lie-to-truth takeover, and change markers on every beat. Directional picks for the open notes make this feel ready to outline, and I want that next step.

### G — 8/10
Elegant and specific where many sheets stay generic: ice-world call sign, listening-not-forcing translation craft, communicator as the theme made physical. Doubled abandonment (father and matriarch) has torque; industrialists still need a face and a prize.

### H — 10/10
The one I most want to see made. Dual-track spine with offset lows, love-as-tracking-device irony, Jonah’s paid cost, voluntary-passage Midpoint that mutates the goal, unsolved signal class planted for a testimony climax, and a mother who earns a third act. Decisions are closed with reasons; the story is not just complete, it is *designed*.

### I — 9/10
Sharp prose and a sound spine: Midpoint house and All Is Lost choice cleanly split, ASI as argument for letting go, mother gap honestly named. Structural assessment alone shows development maturity; Fun and Games still needs a pursuer from page 30.

### J — 8/10
Mistranslation plant (family stays together vs. family survives distance) is a smart theme engine, and the public-authentication climax makes the work concrete. Story holds; consortium still feels like generic pressure without a human face.

### K — 7/10
Capable pursuit-and-battle architecture with a clear father footprint Midpoint and harness-as-climax tool. Dark Night leans on an ASI monologue that states the theme rather than forcing Nia to discover it; interesting enough, less want-more than H/L/F/I.

### L — 9/10
Near-equal to H on development value: Listening Reef, held-vs-anchored mistranslation that costs the crew, consortium alerted by *her* certainty, and a consent climax where nobody speaks for the whale. Causality chain is the tightest after H; I want the sequence outline.

### M — 5/10
Bones of the same premise, but the beat map fights the story: false-victory Midpoint finds Bali early, father reveal lands at All Is Lost, and Fun and Games is “each planet is an adventure.” Ending (Nia puts down roots for a cycle) is a choice, yet the middle never earns the want to know more.
