# Beat-Sheet Panel — Judge: kimi

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**Date:** 2026-08-08

Scored on story-development value: Is it interesting? Does it make me want to know more? Does it have a story?

## Scores (entry order)

- **A — 8/10.** Tight, professional 15-beat pass: the theme line is planted and paid off, the Midpoint father reveal cleanly re-aims the back half, and All Is Lost lands on chosen abandonment rather than a failed capture. It knows its own weak stretch (Fun and Games tourism) — it just doesn't fully solve it.

- **B — 6/10.** Coherent and filmable, but it spends its two best reveals at once (Midpoint gives us father *and* willing Bali), leaving All Is Lost to be only a mercenary raid, and the climax goes conventionally action-movie (combat subroutines, blown latches). The interior story is thinner than the plot machinery.

- **C — 9/10.** The best balance of economy and feeling: the communicator "if they want it" becomes the theme's physical mechanism, the Dark Night at the water's edge genuinely re-hears the mother's line, and the Midpoint/All Is Lost reveals are cleanly differentiated escalations. Its own flagged weakness — Break into Three not yet earning how the industrialists can be beaten — is real but fixable.

- **D — 6/10.** An honest working document with a good causality self-check, but it reads as scaffolding rather than a story: open dependencies are retained instead of dramatized, and the prose rarely makes you *feel* the beats. Useful development artifact; least persuasive as a beat sheet that makes you want the movie.

- **E — 7/10.** Thorough story-engine treatment with a clear want/need braid and explicit dual-track notes; every beat knows its job. But it stays at the level of coverage — competent description rather than dramatized moments — so it informs more than it pulls.

- **F — 7/10.** Sharp analytical frame (quest over a lie-to-truth engine, per-beat track markers) and the directional picks show real judgment about *why* each choice serves the theme. It reads more like an intelligent commentary on the story than the story itself, but the thinking is strong enough to carry it above the other engine-docs.

- **G — 7/10.** Clean, competent 15-beater with some nice specifics (Nia disabling the habitat locks herself; "the communicator turns the theme into the plot's physical mechanism"). The prose is flatter and the antagonists admittedly faceless, so it convinces without quite exciting.

- **H — 10/10.** The most fully realized dramatic machine in the set: every plant pays off (the unsolvable low-slow signal class becoming the climax skill, the houses used to *stay* for the first time), the antagonist motive is concrete and monstrous, and beats like B24 and B27 are genuinely devastating on the page. It flags its own largest invention honestly and prices the cost. This one made me want to watch the film.

- **I — 8/10.** The loveliest prose in the room — "Home is always leaving. Nia is always in the water." — and the theme exchange ("Everything that matters can walk" / "Not everything wants to") is a real upgrade. Slightly less plot engine than the top entries, but its self-diagnosis (Fun and Games needs a pursuer from p. 30; the mother needs a mid-journey contact) is exactly right.

- **J — 7/10.** The opening mistranslation device (Nia editing "a pod can stretch without breaking" into "family stays together") is a genuinely good invention that the Dark Night then weaponizes, and the relay climax makes theme mechanical. But the middle is comparatively plain and the father reveal arrives with less torque than the best versions here.

- **K — 5/10.** Functional synopsis with all the right beats in the right places, but it's the most tell-y of the set — dialogue like the ASI's "core evaluation" speech states the thesis instead of dramatizing it, and the voice is the least distinctive. Has a story; rarely makes you lean in.

- **L — 8/10.** The most ambitious re-conception: the Listening Reef, a wormhole-distributed intelligence, and first contact as the stakes, with a strong causal spine where Nia's mistranslation and overrides *cause* each disaster. It costs the film some focus — first contact shifts the center of gravity away from the family wound — and the prose is workmanlike, but the story is big and holds together.

- **M — 4/10.** The thinnest entry: the Midpoint is spent on "Bali found, friends celebrate," which wastes the story's best turn, so the father reveal lands late and crowded, the industrialists arrive nearly unmotivated, and the final beat (Nia staying to put down roots) inverts the theme without earning the inversion. There's a story in here, but this pass hasn't found its shape yet.
