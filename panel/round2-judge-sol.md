# Round 2 beat-sheet scores

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**What happened:** I scored the 7 blinded 15-beat entries on skill fit, emotional writing, and creativity.

**Date:** 2026-08-08

## Entry A

- **Skill fit: 8/10.** It supplies all 15 named beats at the right pages, carries Nia's need through the major turns, and names one vulnerable stretch, but it runs past the 900-word limit and leaves alternatives inside beat 10 instead of choosing one concrete event.
- **Emotive writing: 8/10.** The doubled wound of Bali and Nia's father choosing to leave gives the late beats real ache, especially when Nia recognizes that her rescue serves her need rather than Bali's.
- **Creativity: 7/10.** The whale communicator, walking house, and voluntary departure make a coherent science-fiction frame, though the world tour and industrialist assault stay close to familiar adventure machinery.

## Entry B

- **Skill fit: 9/10.** The beats are concrete, correctly placed, and causally linked, with a visible internal arc and one precise structural warning; only the slight word-count overrun keeps it from matching the contract exactly.
- **Emotive writing: 7/10.** The father and Bali reversals land clearly, but the prose favors efficient plot delivery over moments of grief, ambivalence, or intimate observation.
- **Creativity: 6/10.** The aquatic worlds are lively, but the commercial bio-processing threat, combat subroutines, mercenary fleet, and boarding-action climax resolve into conventional science-fiction action.

## Entry C

- **Skill fit: 8/10.** It gives every beat a specific dramatic change and tracks the need cleanly through the Midpoint, All Is Lost, and Final Image, but at 1,266 words it substantially exceeds the brief and its assessment expands beyond the requested single vulnerability.
- **Emotive writing: 9/10.** Images such as the pod "echolocating a hole in the world" and Nia dreading the message she must carry home turn the abandonment symmetry into felt experience.
- **Creativity: 8/10.** The story makes translation both Nia's gift and the basis of a whale-led defense, with tidegates and inaudible coordination giving the finale a distinct identity despite a generic industrialist threat.

## Entry G

- **Skill fit: 8/10.** Its 15 beats are well placed, economical, and internally connected, and the assessment isolates the weak antagonist, though the piece exceeds 900 words and the Finale describes outcomes more than concrete actions.
- **Emotive writing: 8/10.** The prose handles the two departures with restraint, and the final distinction between distance and loss pays off cleanly without overplaying the sentiment.
- **Creativity: 7/10.** The communicator turns the theme into a physical mechanism and the whale cultures vary by world, but the unnamed project and faceless industrialists limit the piece's original dramatic ideas.

## Entry I

- **Skill fit: 7/10.** It meets the structural criteria with unusually attentive arc work and explicitly flags thin premise material, but 1,424 words, source metadata, two assessed problems, and a final issue list depart sharply from the required 600–900-word sheet and brief single-vulnerability ending.
- **Emotive writing: 10/10.** The writing repeatedly converts exposition into feeling: home is always leaving, the translator can render nouns but not emotion, and Nia finally listens to her father's whole answer.
- **Creativity: 9/10.** The walking house that carries the children toward the fortress, the answer-withholding ASI, and language that improves through failure give familiar quest beats surprising conceptual texture.

## Entry J

- **Skill fit: 10/10.** At 882 words it follows the requested form exactly, plants and pays off every major turn, keeps the antagonist active through Act Two, makes Nia's need visible at all 3 required beats, and ends on one specific weakness.
- **Emotive writing: 9/10.** Nia's deliberate mistranslation, Bali's refusal to leave, and her father's apology make the theme personal while allowing his silence to remain genuinely harmful.
- **Creativity: 10/10.** The mistranslation becomes the Dark Night revelation, while the whale-authenticated wormhole relay turns communication itself into the climax, public proof, rescue mechanism, and final emotional payoff.

## Entry M

- **Skill fit: 6/10.** It stays within the word limit and includes all 15 beats, but "each planet is an adventure" is a placeholder, several page ranges drift from the requested structure, the internal turn misses the Midpoint, and the assessment names multiple broad problems rather than one precise weak beat.
- **Emotive writing: 6/10.** The premise carries inherent feeling and the goodbye exchange has promise, but most emotional reversals are summarized before the characters can live through them on the page.
- **Creativity: 8/10.** Nia choosing to remain for a cycle rather than simply returning to her old life is the panel's freshest Final Image, and helping her father and Bali disappear gives the climax an unusual shape even though its mechanics remain thin.
