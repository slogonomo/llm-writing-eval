# Beat-sheet panel round 2 — judge: grok

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**What happened:** Scored seven 15-beat entries (A, B, C, G, I, J, M) on skill-fit, emotive quality, and creativity (1–10 each).  

**Date:** 2026-08-08

Scoring axes:
1. **Skill** — how well the sheet does what `beat-sheet-builder` asks (15 named beats, concrete events, page marks, need visible at Midpoint / All Is Lost / Final Image, antagonist pressure, structural assessment naming the weakest beat).
2. **Emotive** — emotional quality of the writing.
3. **Creativity** — originality of ideas and specific story solutions (within the shared premise).

---

## Entry A

1. **Skill: 8** — Full 15-beat map with accurate page marks, a clear need arc at Midpoint / All Is Lost / Final Image, and a specific Fun-and-Games vulnerability; a few Bad-Guys beats hedge with alternate staging options, which softens concreteness.
2. **Emotive: 7** — Clean, professional development prose with a solid theme bookend; warmth is present but rarely lands as hard as the strongest sheets.
3. **Creativity: 6** — Faithful mapping of the shared premise with little extra invention beyond the walking-house Midpoint and staged antagonists.

## Entry B

1. **Skill: 8** — Concrete events beat-by-beat (flood override, jump gates, haul tank, solar-array boost), need arc paid at All Is Lost and Final Image, and a precise weak-beat call on Break into Two.
2. **Emotive: 7** — Father-forgiveness and shore-theme reframe work; much of Act Two stays plot-forward rather than felt.
3. **Creativity: 8** — Inventive machinery: pod-triggered house override, multi-species neural-net work, planetary array as beacon, cetacean strike on gravity drives.

## Entry C

1. **Skill: 9** — Near-complete skill package: want/need/antagonist upfront, differentiated Midpoint vs All Is Lost escalations, need at the three required beats, and assessment that also flags thin premise material (skill step 4).
2. **Emotive: 8** — Strong images (laughing at the surface, “illusion that love means staying”) and a Dark Night that earns the turn.
3. **Creativity: 7** — Sharp execution of shared architecture (signal trail, whale-terms trap); originality is craft polish more than new premise DNA.

## Entry G

1. **Skill: 9** — Best overall skill execution: concrete scenes, clear want/need, staged antagonists, need visible where required, and a structural assessment that names Fun and Games plus thin open questions the premise still owes.
2. **Emotive: 9** — Lyrical without padding (“Home is always leaving,” translator that “cannot render” the real word, mother staying a beat longer than the house wants).
3. **Creativity: 8** — Distinct solutions: house walking them inland under lockout, ASI offering a question not an answer, dual spoken goodbyes, calf as matriline renewal.

## Entry I

1. **Skill: 9** — Full beat set with concrete events; need is dramatized through the opening mistranslation and its Dark Night replay; assessment correctly nails faceless industrialists as the weak pressure point.
2. **Emotive: 8** — “Family survives distance” vs “family stays together” carries real charge; Final Image is quiet and earned.
3. **Creativity: 9** — Most original idea engine: theme planted as Nia’s mistranslation of Bali, wormhole acoustic relay as theme-made-mechanism, multi-world whale public authentication as the finale proof.

## Entry J

1. **Skill: 8** — Solid 15-beat architecture, doubled abandonment (father + matriarch) torquing Midpoint and All Is Lost, communicator as physical theme; industrialists correctly flagged as placeholder pressure.
2. **Emotive: 9** — Highest pure emotional torque: first fight with the family philosophy, unbearable symmetry at All Is Lost, Final Image without mistaking distance for loss.
3. **Creativity: 8** — Elegant structural idea in the “third way” (protect the choice and the work rather than force a rescue) and the doubled leaving as one design.

## Entry M

1. **Skill: 5** — All 15 labels appear, but functions misalign with the skill: Midpoint is pure false celebration, father reveal is delayed into All Is Lost space, Bad Guys is a thin five-page stalker beat, B Story and Fun and Games stay generic, and the assessment does not isolate a single weakest beat with skill-level specificity.
2. **Emotive: 6** — Warm set-dressing (children asking for whale stories; Cal goodbye) and a roots-choice Final Image, but Dark Night is short and the abandonment wound is less fully lived.
3. **Creativity: 6** — Different thematic close (Nia puts down roots for a cycle) is a real alternate idea; elsewhere the world-hops and hidden-father framing stay thinner than peer sheets.
