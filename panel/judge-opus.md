# Blind panel scores — beat sheets, judge: opus

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**What happened:** I read the 13 blind entries (A–M) and scored each 1–10 as story development work.

**Date:** 2026-08-08

I judged on three questions: is it interesting, does it make me want to know more, does it have a story. I rewarded entries that *developed* the material — invented causality, plants and payoffs, and a reason the climax must be this climax — over entries that restated the premise cleanly.

## Scores in entry order

**A — 8/10.** Tight, well-shaped 15 beats with real prose economy; "Nothing clean left to bring home" is the best one-line summary of All Is Lost in the set. It develops the material honestly but adds little the premise didn't already contain, and its own assessment correctly names Fun and Games as tourism.

**B — 6/10.** Clean and readable, but Act Three collapses into generic action — a two-pronged strike, a corporate commander, a released latch. The theme is announced in the Dark Night rather than dramatised, so the ending pays a debt the audience never watched accrue.

**C — 8/10.** Strong story engine up front, the two reveals (father, then Bali's choice) are cleanly separated escalations, and the writing is confident. It also diagnoses its own weakest joint — Nia's pivot from rescuer to defender happens in one unearned decision — which is right, and unfixed.

**D — 6/10.** A careful working document rather than a beat sheet: bracketed open questions, variant paths, a self-check appendix. Genuinely useful to the next writer, but it holds the story at arm's length and never commits, so it reads as inventory, not drama.

**E — 7/10.** Same spine as A with more apparatus — dual-track notes, want/need articulation, retained open decisions. The internal/external cross at the Midpoint is well argued, but the prose is denser than A's for the same content, and denseness costs a beat sheet its readability.

**F — 7/10.** The explicit *therefore* chaining and the per-beat "changes: knowledge / pressure / relationship" tags make the causality auditable, which I liked. Its invention — the father's work as a fixed bridge project — is flagged as directional filler rather than made load-bearing, so the reveal explains without detonating.

**G — 7.5/10.** The best line-level writing in the set: "presses a communicator into Bali's absence" does character, plot, and theme in six words. It is honest that the industrialists have no face and no specific prize, and that honesty is also the ceiling — its antagonist is still a placeholder at page 75.

**H — 10/10.** The only entry that behaves like a second draft rather than a first. It plants an unsolved signal class in beat 3 and pays it off as the climax (whale calls encode duration, not words — the thought is long, so the signal is long), makes the industrialists hunt *Nia* rather than Bali so her love is literally the tracking device, gives the mother a third act where her walking houses finally *stay*, and wins the climax on a legal point — you cannot salvage a witness — that the whole film has been building the capability for. It also declares its largest departure from the source notes and says how to cut it back.

**I — 7.5/10.** Well-written and structurally alert; the Dark Night where the ASI offers a question instead of an answer is the best use of that character in the set. Its two named problems — an episodic Fun and Games and a mother who vanishes for 85 pages — are correctly identified but left as notes rather than fixed in the beats.

**J — 8/10.** The mistranslation plant is a real invention: Nia renders Bali's "a pod can stretch without breaking" as "family stays together" in beat 2, and the ASI replays it in the Dark Night. That is theme, character flaw, and reversal in one device. The acoustic relay makes the climax mechanically specific too; the consortium remains thin.

**K — 6/10.** Complete and coherent, but written as an operations report — neural harnesses, transducer arrays, bio-sonar filters, interdiction passes. The emotional turns are asserted in summary ("Nia realizes...") rather than staged, and the ASI's Dark Night speech states the theme outright instead of letting a scene carry it.

**L — 9/10.** The most thematically ambitious entry. It converts the story into a fight over consent — "nobody speaks for the whale" — where Nia wins by making Bali audible rather than by rescuing her, and it refuses to let the father off: secrecy protected the mission *and* spared him from facing the people he hurt. The mistranslation of "held" versus "anchored by choice" costs the crew a jump, which is exactly how a middle act should escalate. Its 21 sequence-beats occasionally read as outline rather than beat sheet.

**M — 5.5/10.** Sketchy and structurally loose — All Is Lost sits where the Midpoint should, Fun and Games is one sentence, and the finale hands the whale back to the plot. But it has the one genuinely surprising ending in the set: Nia stays and puts down roots, the choice her father made, and the goodbye lands on Cal instead of the pod. That instinct is worth more than the draft around it.

## Rank order, best first

1. **H** — plants and pays off, invents the antagonist's motive, gives every family member an arc.
2. **L** — consent as the story's real subject; the most morally honest ending.
3. **J** — one strong invented device (the mistranslation) carries theme and climax.
4. **C** — confident engine, cleanly separated reveals, honest about its unearned pivot.
5. **A** — tightest, most readable version of the baseline story.
6. **G** — best prose, weakest antagonist.
7. **I** — alert and well-written; problems diagnosed but not fixed.
8. **E** — thorough dual-track analysis, denser than it needs to be.
9. **F** — auditable causality, filler-grade central invention.
10. **B** — clean until Act Three turns generic.
11. **K** — complete but inert; summary in place of scene.
12. **D** — a good working document, not yet a story.
13. **M** — undercooked, but the only entry with a surprise in it.
