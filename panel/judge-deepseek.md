# Judge: deepseek — beat-sheet panel, entries A–M

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**Date:** 2026-08-08

**What happened:** Scored all 13 blind entries (A–M) on interest, pull, and story integrity.

Scores are in entry order. One or two sentences of reasoning each. Rank order at the end, best first.

## Scores

**A — 8/10.** Clean, competent, by-the-book 15-beat execution: the mother's theme line is planted and re-meant, and All Is Lost lands on chosen abandonment rather than a capture fail. It is the safest telling of the set, and its own assessment honestly flags the Fun and Games stretch as tourism risk.

**B — 7/10.** Solid, conventional quest with a visceral All Is Lost (doors blown, research core stolen, Bali winched into a haul tank). The theme stays statement rather than engine, and the whale-assisted escape via flood override is a self-flagged contrivance.

**C — 9/10.** The best pure 15-beat craft here: the "I chose this" All Is Lost reframes the whole quest, the Dark Night re-reads the mother's line as love instead of surrender, and the assessment is honest that Break into Three needs a planted capability. Warm and specific; it makes you want to see the fight on the whales' terms.

**D — 6/10.** Reads like a development scaffold rather than a beat sheet: 18 beats with open brackets on every mechanism (elevator vs. port, ASI rule, FTL, the father's work, contact blackout). The causality self-check is careful, but there is no voice and little momentum — it tells you what is unresolved, not what happens.

**E — 8/10.** A prose sibling of A (same opening image, same spacer, same beats) with a clean dual-track spine. Better writing — "she has never stress-tested the belief that both her families will stay" — but weaker structural presentation, with no pages and no numbered beats.

**F — 7/10.** Thorough and well-caused: B12's pursuers follow from B14's hidden work, and the communicator pays off B5. Reads like a careful development memo, dry, and the emotional beats do not lift off the page.

**G — 8/10.** Tight and concrete: the ice-world whales remembering the tanker's call sign, "her rig, built to translate whale speech, cannot touch it," and a Dark Night that states both horns cleanly. Slightly conventional, and the industrialists remain a faceless placeholder.

**H — 9/10.** The most fully realized entry. The unsolved signal class (whales encode duration, not words) turns the climax into a skill payoff; "her love is the tracking device" makes Act Three inevitable; the mother gets a real third act. Dense at 37 beats, but this is the one with a genuine dramatic engine.

**I — 9/10.** The best writing in the set: "the word for missing, then a word it cannot render at all," the "if she wants it" communicator, the ASI's question in the Dark Night. Lovely bookends; the antagonist pressure arrives later than it should, and the sheet knows it.

**J — 8/10.** The most elegant single device of the batch: the opening mistranslation — Bali says family survives distance, Nia hears family stays together — paid off mechanically at the Dark Night. "The man who left Nia has taken her other mother" is the set's best midpoint line. The finale is more generic than the premise deserves.

**K — 7/10.** Dense and competent with nice texture (Nia keeps a physical log of her father's untracked movements; the ASI's "a line back to them"). The prose is report-flat, and "Departure isn't deletion" is the weakest rendering of the theme line.

**L — 9/10.** The most thematically ambitious: a real central question (can Nia defend someone she loves without turning love into captivity), a consent-based climax where nobody speaks for the whale, and an anti-tourism discipline — every world corrects one of Nia's assumptions. Cooler and denser than its rivals, but the moral architecture is the strongest in the set.

**M — 8/10.** The most original voice: Bali's early furtiveness is planted, the theme arrives over coffee, the midpoint is a celebration before the fall, and the ending inverts the premise — Nia, not her father, chooses roots. The middle is thin, the bad guys stay faceless, and it knows it; the beach goodbye is the most memorable image of the batch.
