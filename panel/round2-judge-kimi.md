# Beat-sheet panel, round 2 — judge: kimi

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**Date:** 2026-08-08

**What happened:** Scored all seven 15-beat entries on three axes, 1–10 each: (1) fidelity to the beat-sheet-builder skill, (2) emotive quality of the writing, (3) creativity of the ideas.

---

## Entry A

1. **Skill fidelity: 9** — All 15 beats named and correctly placed, every beat a concrete event, theme visible at Midpoint/All Is Lost/Final Image, and the structural assessment names the single weakest beat (Fun and Games) with a specific reason.
2. **Emotive quality: 9** — Dense, felt writing ("father is absence with no farewell"; "the symmetry is unbearable" territory without saying it); the All Is Lost beat lands on chosen abandonment rather than mechanics, which is the emotional core done right.
3. **Creativity: 9** — The doubled departure (matriarch leaving the pod the way the father left the family) plus the translator as the instrument of the theme are genuinely original story devices, not decoration.

## Entry B

1. **Skill fidelity: 8** — Full 15-beat spine with accurate markers and a named weakest beat, though several beats read as synopsis report ("leads a two-pronged strike") rather than concrete scenes.
2. **Emotive quality: 6** — Competent but flat; the All Is Lost beat is externalized into mercenary action, so the internal wound gets less page-time than in the stronger entries, and the closing line is carried over nearly verbatim.
3. **Creativity: 6** — Solid execution of the shared premise but the most conventional take: the father's reveal is explained away early and the third act resolves with standard assault-and-board mechanics.

## Entry C

1. **Skill fidelity: 9** — Beats all present, concrete, and correctly marked; assessment identifies Break into Three with a specific fix and honestly flags the premise-thin beats per the skill's step 4.
2. **Emotive quality: 8** — Strong, controlled prose ("a false peak that collapses into fury"); the Dark Night beat, where Nia re-hears her mother's sentence as love that doesn't chain, is the emotional peak of the sheet.
3. **Creativity: 8** — The corporate vessel logging her route as a quiet Fun-and-Games intercut is a smart structural invention; otherwise it shares the common premise DNA without adding as much new as A or J.

## Entry G

1. **Skill fidelity: 8** — All beats present and concrete with accurate markers; the assessment's weakest-beat call (faceless industrialists) is sharp, though a few beats (Break into Two) lean toward compression.
2. **Emotive quality: 8** — Lean and elegant ("presses a communicator into Bali's absence"); the Dark Night dilemma, where both choices feel like abandonment, is genuinely painful in two sentences.
3. **Creativity: 8** — The communicator as the theme made physical mechanism, and the second family "who stay not because they have to, but because they choose to," are fresh articulations of the shared material.

## Entry I

1. **Skill fidelity: 9** — Everything the skill asks plus more: concrete beats, accurate markers, a weakest-beat call with a remedy, and an explicit list of premise-thin beats per skill step 4.
2. **Emotive quality: 9** — The best line-level writing of the set ("echolocating a hole in the world"; the translator that "renders nouns and fumbles feeling"); emotion is dramatized through the translation device rather than stated.
3. **Creativity: 9** — Invents a new Theme Stated line ("Everything that matters can walk" / "Not everything wants to") and an ASI whose withheld answers embody the argument for letting go — theme encoded in character rules.

## Entry J

1. **Skill fidelity: 8** — All 15 beats present, concrete, correctly placed, with a clean weakest-beat assessment; slightly thinner scene texture than the top tier but nothing missing.
2. **Emotive quality: 8** — Clean and affecting, with the Dark Night built around the ASI replaying Nia's own mistranslation; plainer prose than A/C/I but the emotional logic is airtight.
3. **Creativity: 9** — The most original single device in the set: Nia's opening mistranslation ("family stays together" for "family survives distance") plants the theme as a plot object, and the wormhole relay makes the theme the literal climax mechanism.

## Entry M

1. **Skill fidelity: 5** — Roughly half the target length; several beats are placeholders ("Each planet is an adventure"); page markers drift (Midpoint at p. 60, a five-page Bad Guys Close In); the assessment is vague and mislabels the antagonist as "hidden protagonist."
2. **Emotive quality: 7** — Where it commits, it works: the Theme Stated scene over coffee ("Someday they won't, or you won't") and the final beach exchange with Cal carry real feeling in plain dialogue.
3. **Creativity: 7** — The closing inversion — Nia choosing to stay and put down roots while the community moves on — is a genuinely different ending from the rest of the field, and the granddaughter's granddaughters beat is a nice mirror; the ideas outrun the execution.
