# Blind Beat-Sheet Scores — Judge Sol

> Research commentary is MIT licensed. Creative quotations and story expression are excluded; see [LICENSE](../LICENSE). Known evaluation errors are documented in the [audit](../RESULTS_AUDIT.md).

**What happened:** L and H lead this ballot; both turn Nia's mistake into the third-act crisis and make Bali's agency the solution.<br>

**Date:** 2026-08-08

## Scores

### Entry A — 8.0/10

The father reveal and Bali's voluntary departure form a strong emotional one-two punch, and the communicator gives the ending a clean payoff. The worlds, the father's work, and the industrialist assault remain too generic for the second half to feel inevitable.

### Entry B — 7.9/10

This is a clear, propulsive rescue story with legible stakes and a workable action climax. It loses some emotional complexity by explaining the father's absence too neatly and resolving the conflict through a fairly standard fleet battle.

### Entry C — 8.5/10

The quest and abandonment wound interlock cleanly, while Bali's choice lands as a painful reversal rather than a plot trick. The finale still depends on an undefined project and a vague promise to fight "on the whales' terms."

### Entry D — 7.2/10

The emotional spine is sound, especially the symmetry between Nia's father and Bali. Too many decisive story choices remain open, however, and the industrialists and final confrontation read as placeholders rather than developed drama.

### Entry E — 7.5/10

This version understands the theme and maps the internal and external tracks with precision. It is more persuasive as structural analysis than as a story because the father's work, antagonists, travel complications, and climax remain abstract.

### Entry F — 7.7/10

The pursuit has a clean progression, and hints that the supposed monster is careful rather than cruel add useful tension. The worlds are still mostly lore stops, while the father's bridge project and the triumphant confrontation lack enough concrete action to create suspense.

### Entry G — 8.2/10

This is concise, emotionally coherent, and especially strong when Bali refuses rescue and turns Nia's love into the danger she brought with her. The industrialists have no specific face or objective, so the external climax cannot yet match the internal one.

### Entry H — 9.3/10

This version delivers the most rigorous setup-and-payoff chain: the unsolved whale signal, Nia's mistranslation, her pursuit becoming the enemy's tracker, and Bali speaking for herself all converge in one climax. A few mechanisms, especially the salvage-law shortcut and the mother's perfectly timed arrival, feel engineered, but the story has exceptional momentum and emotional payoff.

### Entry I — 8.3/10

The writing is vivid, the walking-house reveal is cinematic, and the repeated image of Nia almost understanding Bali carries real feeling. The episodic middle and the generic industrialist battle leave the climax less distinctive than the setup promises.

### Entry J — 8.8/10

The opening mistranslation pays off beautifully, and the wormhole relay turns the theme of surviving distance into the actual mechanism of victory. The consortium still needs a human face and sharper commercial motive, but this is a compact, compelling movie spine.

### Entry K — 6.8/10

The plot is specific and easy to follow, with a usable translation-relay payoff. It leans on familiar mercenary action, an on-the-nose ASI lesson, and technical explanation where sharper character choices could carry the emotion.

### Entry L — 9.5/10

This is the richest story: every clue changes Nia's belief, her own certainty exposes the refuge, and the first-contact climax resolves consent, ownership, family, and whale communication at once. It is dense and may need compression, but its moral complications stay alive rather than excusing the adults, which makes me want to see the film.

### Entry M — 5.8/10

The core father-and-whale reversal survives, and Nia's final choice to put down roots offers a fresh image. The midpoint is confusing, the pursuers arrive without groundwork, and the adventure, antagonist, father's work, and climax are too thin to sustain a feature.
